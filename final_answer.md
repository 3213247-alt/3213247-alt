### Step 1: The Program Exists
- **School**: University at Albany School of Public Health

- **Accreditation**: CEPH (Council on Education for Public Health)
- **Degrees Offered**:

  - MS in Biostatistics
  - MPH with Epidemiology concentration

### Step 2: It Meets Your Depth Requirement (10+ Year Learning Path)

**Years 1-2**: Master's degree (statistical theory, epidemiologic methods)
**Years 3-4**: CPH certification + specialization (infectious/chronic/environmental)
**Years 5-6**: Advanced methods (Bayesian, machine learning, causal inference)
**Years 7-8**: Domain expertise (spatial epidemiology, network analysis)
**Years 9-10**: Leadership skills (grant writing, policy translation)
**Years 10+**: Continuous learning (new diseases, methods, regulations)

Total: 5,000+ hours of progressive learning, comparable to actuarial exams

- **Academia**: Epidemiology professors among highest paid in public health
- **International**: WHO recruits epidemiologists globally
- **Salary progression**: $70K → $120K → $180K+ over 20 years

Post-COVID reality:
- 70% of epidemiology positions offer remote/hybrid
- CDC allows full remote for many positions

- Data analysis doesn't require physical presence

### Step 5: International Mobility Confirmed

- **Geneva**: WHO headquarters
- **Atlanta**: CDC (global influence)
- **London**: LSHTM positions
- **Any country**: National health agencies need epidemiologists
- **No licensure barriers**: Skills transfer globally

### Step 6: Low Automation Risk

Why it can't be automated:
- Requires human judgment for study design
- Ethical considerations need human oversight
- Policy recommendations require context
- Stakeholder communication essential
- Causal inference requires expertise

### Step 7: 20-Year Tenure Common

Examples of long careers:
- Dr. Anthony Fauci: 50+ years at NIH
- CDC epidemiologists routinely work 30+ years
- Academic epidemiologists often work into 70s
- Government positions have strong job security

### Step 8: Little to No Burnout

Why burnout is rare:
- Regular hours (not clinical medicine)
- Intellectually stimulating (always new problems)
- Meaningful work (save lives)
- Collaborative culture
- Work-life balance respected

### Step 9: Growth From Both Movement and Stability

**If you move**: Each country/outbreak adds to reputation
**If you stay**: Become institutional expert, mentor others
Both paths lead to respect

### Step 10: The Balance Between Specialized and Broad

**Specialized enough**: 
- Deep statistical knowledge
- Specific disease expertise
- Regulatory mastery

**Broad enough**:
- Can switch disease focus
- Can move between sectors
- Can change geographic focus
