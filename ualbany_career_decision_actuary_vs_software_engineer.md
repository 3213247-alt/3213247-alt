## Step 9: Decision Framework

### Choose ACTUARY if:

**Personal Traits:**
1. You are in top 10% mathematically (Calc I-III grades: A or A-)
2. You have extreme discipline (can study 15-20 hours/week for 7 years while working)
3. You are risk-averse (prefer stability over high variance outcomes)
4. You don't mind repetitive studying (taking 7-10 exams over decade)

**Life Preferences:**
5. You are willing to relocate to Hartford CT or other insurance hub
6. You value work-life balance AFTER age 30 (not before)
7. You want career that gets better with age (no age discrimination)
8. You prefer structured career path (pass exams = promotions)

**Test This:**
- Take Exam P practice test (free online: Coaching Actuaries sample test)
- Can you solve 20-25% of problems with no preparation? → Consider actuary
- Can you solve <10% of problems? → Actuary will be very difficult

**If 6+ of above are TRUE:** Actuary is better choice for you

---

### Choose SOFTWARE ENGINEER if:

**Personal Traits:**
1. You like building things and solving concrete problems
2. You learn new things quickly and enjoy technology
3. You are comfortable with uncertainty and change
4. You don't want to study for exams for a decade

**Life Preferences:**
5. You want to stay near Albany or have location flexibility
6. You value work-life balance from day 1 (not just after 10 years)
7. You want higher earning potential (willing to accept less stability)
8. You prefer self-directed learning over structured exam system

**Test This:**
- Complete a beginner coding course (FreeCodeCamp: 50 hours)
- Do you enjoy it and want to keep learning? → Consider software engineer
- Does it feel tedious and boring? → Software engineering will be hard

**If 6+ of above are TRUE:** Software Engineer is better choice for you

---

### Neither is Good Choice if:

1. You are average at math AND average at coding (choose business, accounting, other field)
2. You want easy path with minimal effort (both require significant work)
3. You need certainty and low risk (consider accounting, healthcare, trades)
4. You value creative expression over analytical work (consider design, marketing, arts)
