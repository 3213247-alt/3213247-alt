# Actuarial Career Market Viability for Career Changers at Age 35+
## Analysis Based on Private Sector Recruiting Firm Reports (2024-2025)

---

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes the actuarial career market viability for individuals aged 35+ considering a career change, based on data from private sector recruiting firms including DW Simpson, Ezra Penland, and Actuarial Careers, Inc. The analysis covers current market conditions and the 7-10 year outlook (2025-2035).

---

## Source: DW Simpson 2025 Market Trends Report

### Current Market Reality (2024-2025)

**Market Conditions:**
- 2024 was a "challenging year in the actuarial recruiting market"
- Slowdown in hiring continued from the second half of 2023 into 2024
- Economic concerns, return-to-office policies, and reduced flexibility in hiring remote candidates contributed to the slower market
- Employment growth remained positive but at a slower pace than the previous 2 years
- Salaries continued to increase, but wage growth aligned more with pre-pandemic levels

**Job Market Statistics:**
- Actuarial unemployment rate: under 1%
- Projected job growth: 22% from 2023-2033 (compared to 4% for all occupations)
- Actuaries rank among top positions for: Best Business Jobs, Best STEM Jobs, Top Paying Jobs, Best Jobs Overall

## What Has Changed: Exams Alone Are No Longer Sufficient

### The Old Model (Pre-2020)
- Passing actuarial exams was the primary requirement for entry
- Career changers could enter the field primarily through exam progress
- Technical programming skills were secondary

### The New Model (2025 and Beyond)

**Required Technical Skills Per DW Simpson 2025 Report:**
- AI and machine learning expertise
- Data analytics capabilities
- Python and programming proficiency
- Predictive analytics skills
- Customer data analysis
- Machine learning integration for risk modeling and pricing

**Quote from DW Simpson 2025 Market Trends:**
> "Actuaries who combine traditional expertise with cutting-edge technical skills in AI, machine learning, and data analytics will be best positioned to excel."

> "Automation and artificial intelligence are expected to play an increasingly prominent role in pricing, risk modeling, and claims forecasting as actuaries integrate AI and machine learning into their work to enhance efficiency and accuracy. This will result in a growing demand for expertise in data analytics and technological innovation, alongside traditional actuarial training."

## Career Changer Considerations at Age 35+

### Current Hiring Environment Challenges

**Per DW Simpson Client Insights:**
- "The actuarial job market in 2025 remains highly competitive, with an exceptionally low unemployment rate"
- 40% of professionals changed jobs during or shortly after the pandemic
- Over 70% of actuaries prefer remote or hybrid roles
- Housing market and high mortgage rates (6-7%) deter relocation

### Generational Workforce Composition

**DW Simpson data on workforce demographics:**
- Millennials and Gen Z now comprise nearly 60% of the actuarial workforce
- Baby Boomers showing stronger preference for traditional in-office roles
- Generation Alpha (born post-2010) reaching internship and early career stages

**This means:**
- Career changers at 35+ will be competing against a younger workforce that is:
  - More technologically adept
  - Self-reliant with digital tools
  - Willing to accept lower starting salaries
  - More flexible on work arrangements

### What Employers Actually Value (Per DW Simpson)

1. Work-life balance flexibility
2. Remote/hybrid work capability
3. Transparency in DEI initiatives
4. Continuous learning commitment
5. Technical adaptability

## Concrete Assessment: Market Welcome for 35+ Career Changers

### Factors Working Against Age 35+ Entry

1. **Time to Credentialing**: Full FSA/FCAS takes 5-7+ years; entering at 35 means reaching credential at 40-42+

2. **Entry-Level Salary Expectations**: Entry positions pay $65,000-$85,000 (per DW Simpson salary surveys); significant pay cut for most career changers

3. **Technology Gap**: Younger candidates entering now have native AI/ML skills; career changers must acquire these while also studying for exams

4. **Hiring Preferences**: Per DW Simpson, employers seek candidates who "embrace innovation and align their capabilities with industry needs"

5. **Remote Work Paradox**: Remote positions are highly competitive (70%+ preference); on-site positions require relocation that career changers with families may not accept

### Factors Working In Favor

1. **Low Unemployment**: Under 1% unemployment means positions exist

2. **Retirement Wave**: Baby Boomers retiring creates replacement demand

3. **Industry Experience Value**: Prior experience in finance, healthcare, tech, or insurance provides transferable domain knowledge

4. **Niche Specializations**: Climate risk, cyber risk, and healthcare analytics are newer areas where experience matters more than credential tenure

## Specific Recommendations for 35+ Career Changers

### Prerequisites Before Committing

1. **Technical Skills First**: Acquire Python, SQL, R, and machine learning fundamentals before or alongside exam study

2. **Target Growing Sectors**: Focus on health, P&C climate risk, or insurtech rather than traditional life insurance

3. **Leverage Existing Industry**: If currently in healthcare, finance, or tech, target actuarial roles in that same sector

4. **Geographic Flexibility**: Willingness to relocate significantly expands opportunities

5. **Exam Velocity**: Pass 3-4 exams before applying to demonstrate commitment and competence

## Conclusion

### Does the Market Welcome 35+ Newcomers in 2025?

**Short Answer:** The market does not explicitly exclude older career changers, but it does not actively welcome them either. The under-1% unemployment rate indicates positions exist, but competition is intense.

### Will the Market Welcome 35+ Newcomers in 2032-2035?

**Assessment based on current trends:**
- Automation will reduce the number of traditional entry-level positions
- Technical skills requirements will increase further
- Niche specializations (climate risk, cyber, healthcare analytics) may provide openings for career changers with relevant domain expertise
- The 22% job growth projection suggests net positive hiring through 2033

### Bottom Line

For a 35-year-old in 2025 considering this career change:

1. **If you have existing experience in insurance, healthcare, or finance**: The path is more viable because domain knowledge provides value beyond exam progress

2. **If you have strong programming/data science skills**: You address the primary skills gap that employers now require

3. **If you are starting from zero in both domain knowledge and technical skills**: The 7-10 year path to credentialing faces significant headwinds from automation, younger competition, and changing skill requirements

4. **The "pass exams and get hired" model from 30 years ago no longer exists**: Today's market requires technical skills, domain expertise, and exam progress simultaneously
