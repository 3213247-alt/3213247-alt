## STEP 1: EDUCATION REQUIREMENTS - WHAT YOU NEED TO DO

### Path A: Actuary

**Required Coursework at UAlbany**:
- Mathematics (Calculus I, II, III, Linear Algebra)
- Probability and Statistics (multiple courses)
- Economics or Finance courses
- Programming (Python or R)

### Path B: Software Engineer

**Required Coursework at UAlbany**:

- Software Engineering principles
- Databases, Operating Systems
- Electives: Web Development, Mobile Apps, etc.

**Additional Requirements While in School**:
1. Build personal projects (3-5 projects on GitHub)
2. Contribute to open source (optional but helpful)
3. Learn frameworks (React, Angular, Node.js)
4. Technical interview practice (LeetCode problems)
5. GPA requirement: 3.0+ for most opportunities

**Total Self-Study Time During College**: 200-400 hours for projects and interview prep

**CONCRETE DIFFERENCE**: Actuary requires 400-600 hours of exam study ON TOP OF coursework. Software engineering requires project work that can often overlap with coursework.

### Actuary First Job

**Timeline**:
- Apply: Fall senior year (September-December)
- Need: 2-3 exams passed, internship experience, 3.4+ GPA
- Applications: 50-80 companies
- Interviews: 3-8 companies
- Offers: 1-2 if successful
- Job search duration: 3-6 months average

**Starting Salary (2025)**:
- With 2 exams: $65,000-$75,000
- With 3 exams: $70,000-$80,000
- Location: Hartford, Des Moines ($65K-$70K), NYC/Boston ($75K-$85K)

**Work Schedule Year 1**:
- 40-45 hours work per week
- 15-20 hours exam study per week (for next exam)
- Total: 55-65 hours per week

**First Year Reality**:
- Data preparation and cleanup (40% of time)
- Basic calculations and reconciliation (30% of time)
- Learning actuarial software (20% of time)
- Meetings and reports (10% of time)
- Study time for exams: Every evening and weekend

### Software Engineer First Job

**Timeline**:
- Apply: Junior fall through senior spring
- Need: Projects, good GPA (3.0+), strong interview skills
- Applications: 100-200 companies
- Interviews: 10-20 companies
- Offers: 2-4 if successful
- Job search duration: 3-6 months average

**Starting Salary (2025)**:
- Average company: $75,000-$90,000

- FAANG (Google, Meta, etc.): $130,000-$180,000 (total comp)

**Work Schedule Year 1**:
- 40-50 hours work per week
- Self-learning (5-10 hours per week to stay current)
- Total: 45-60 hours per week

**First Year Reality**:
- Bug fixes and small features (40% of time)
- Code reviews and learning codebase (30% of time)
- Meetings and planning (20% of time)
- Documentation and testing (10% of time)
- No mandatory studying, but need to stay current

**CONCRETE DIFFERENCE**: 
- **Salary**: Software engineer starts $10K-$40K higher (depends on company)
- **Hours**: Actuary requires mandatory exam study (extra 15-20 hrs/week)
- **Opportunities**: Software engineering has more jobs available nationwide

## STEP 4: YEARS 2-5 PROGRESSION - MID-EARLY CAREER

### Actuary Career Path

**Exam Progress Required**:
- Year 2: Pass 2 more exams (total: 4-5 exams)
- Year 3: Pass 1-2 more exams (total: 5-6 exams)
- Year 4: Achieve ASA (Associate of Society of Actuaries) designation
- Year 5: Continue toward FSA (Fellowship)

**Study Commitment Years 2-5**:
- 15-20 hours per week studying
- Every weekend spent studying
- Vacation time used for exam study
- Social life significantly impacted
- 300+ hours per exam, multiple exams per year

**Salary Progression**:
- Year 2: $75,000-$85,000 (with 4 exams)
- Year 3: $85,000-$95,000 (with 5 exams)
- Year 4: $95,000-$110,000 (with ASA)
- Year 5: $105,000-$125,000 (ASA + experience)

**Work-Life Balance**:
- 40-45 hour work weeks
- BUT: 15-20 hours exam study = 55-65 total hours
- Exam seasons: Minimal social life
- Between exams: Better balance
- Overall: Moderate to poor balance until exams complete

**Failure Rate**:
- 60% who start actuarial path do NOT complete fellowship
- 40% complete ASA but stop
- 30% leave profession before ASA
- Each failed exam = 6 months delay + $500-$1,000 cost

### Software Engineer Career Path

**Career Progress**:
- Year 2: Mid-level engineer (at some companies)
- Year 3: Senior engineer (at fast-growth companies) or mid-level (at large companies)
- Year 4: Senior engineer or tech lead
- Year 5: Senior engineer, tech lead, or early manager track

**Skill Development Years 2-5**:
- Learn on the job (no mandatory external study)
- Side projects optional (not required)
- Certifications optional (rarely required)
- 5-10 hours per week staying current (reading, learning new tech)

**Salary Progression**:
- Year 2: $90,000-$120,000
- Year 3: $105,000-$140,000
- Year 4: $120,000-$160,000
- Year 5: $135,000-$180,000+
- FAANG track: Add $30K-$80K to all numbers

**Work-Life Balance**:
- 40-50 hour work weeks (company-dependent)
- Some companies: 50-60 hours (startups, crunch times)
- Some companies: 35-45 hours (established, good culture)
- Weekends usually free
- Overall: Good to moderate balance

**Job Security Risk**:
- Tech layoffs happen (2022-2023: 15-20% of industry affected)
- Switching jobs common (average: 2-3 years per company)
- Age discrimination concern (after age 40+)
- H1B visa competition in some markets

**CONCRETE DIFFERENCE**:
- **Salary**: Software engineer earns $100K-$200K more cumulatively in years 1-5
- **Time commitment**: Actuary requires 3,000-4,000 hours of exam study in these years
- **Flexibility**: Software engineer can switch jobs/companies easily; actuary progression tied to exams
- **Stress**: Actuary has exam stress + work; software engineer has work stress but no exams

## STEP 7: FAILURE SCENARIOS - WHAT IF THINGS GO WRONG?

### Actuary Failure Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Can't Pass Exams (30% of starters)**
- Stuck at 2-4 exams after 5 years
- Salary plateaus at $90K-$110K
- Career advancement blocked
- Options:
  - Stay in actuarial support role (lower pay)
  - Pivot to risk management or data analytics
  - Return to school for different career
- **Lost investment**: 2,000-3,000 hours of study time, $5,000-$10,000 in exam fees

**Scenario 2: Can't Get First Job (20% of exam passers)**
- Passed 2-3 exams but no internship or job
- Exams expire (some have time limits on credit)
- Options:
  - Take data analyst role and try to transition
  - Get master's degree to boost credentials
  - Apply to smaller companies or different cities
- **Lost investment**: 1-2 years of job searching, exam progress wasted

**Scenario 3: Hate the Work (15-20% leave voluntarily)**
- Complete some exams, get job, realize it's not fulfilling
- Options:
  - Use quantitative skills to move to data science, risk management, or finance
  - Skills transfer moderately well
  - Starting over in new field at lower level
- **Lost investment**: 3-5 years of career time

### Software Engineer Failure Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Can't Pass Technical Interviews (15-20%)**
- Can code but struggle with algorithm interviews
- Options:
  - Practice more (6-12 months of LeetCode)
  - Target smaller companies with easier interviews
  - Take QA (Quality Assurance) or support role, then transition
- **Lost investment**: 6-12 months of job searching

**Scenario 2: Can't Get First Job (10-15%)**
- Projects not impressive enough or poor interview skills
- Options:
  - Build more substantial projects
  - Contribute to open source
  - Take bootcamp to boost skills
  - Consider contract/freelance work to build experience
- **Lost investment**: 6-12 months of job searching

**Scenario 3: Laid Off / Age Discrimination (20-30% affected at some point)**
- Job loss due to economic downturn or age discrimination
- Options:
  - Find new tech job (easier when younger)
  - Freelance/consulting
  - Move to management
  - Pivot to technical writing, DevOps, or adjacent fields
- **Risk**: Increases significantly after age 40

**CONCRETE DIFFERENCE**:
- **Actuary failure**: Usually exam-related, can see it coming, takes 3-5 years to confirm
- **Software engineer failure**: Usually interview-related or layoff, can happen quickly
- **Recovery**: Software engineer has more adjacent career options; actuary skills are more niche
