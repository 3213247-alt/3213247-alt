## Scenario Analysis: Five Potential Paths

### Scenario 1: "The Exam Grinder" (Passing 1 exam per year, aggressive applications)

**Timeline**: 2029-2035 (6 years)

**Year-by-Year Progression**:
- 2029 (Age 27): 1 exam, 0 internship → Apply to 200+ jobs
- 2030 (Age 28): 2 exams, 0 internship → Apply to 250+ jobs
- 2031 (Age 29): 3 exams, 0 internship → Apply to 300+ jobs
- 2032 (Age 30): 4 exams, 0 internship → Apply to 300+ jobs
- 2033 (Age 31): 5 exams, 0 internship → Apply to 300+ jobs
- 2034 (Age 32): 6 exams, 0 internship → Apply to 300+ jobs
- 2035 (Age 33): 7 exams, still no actuarial job

**Factors Working Against You**:
- Age increases each year (27→33)
- 6-year employment gap in actuarial work
- Hiring managers see "overqualified but no experience"
- Questions arise: "Why hasn't anyone hired you?"
- Younger candidates (22-23) with 2 exams + internship are preferred
- Each failed year compounds negative perception

**Factors Working For You**:
- Exam progression shows dedication
- Technical skills remain relevant
- Eventually may hit 4-5 exams (ASA near-track)

**Cost Analysis**:
- Exam fees: ~$1,400/exam × 6 exams = $8,400
- Study materials: ~$1,000/exam × 6 exams = $6,000
- Lost wages during study time: ~200 hours/exam × $15/hr × 6 = $18,000
- **Total 6-year cost**: $32,400
- **Opportunity cost**: 6 years in low-wage work vs. potential other career paths

**Probability of Success**: **15-25%**

**Reasoning**: 
- By year 3-4 with 4-5 exams, you become interesting to smaller firms desperate for exam progress
- However, age 30-31 with zero actuarial experience is a major red flag
- Most who succeed in this path do so by years 3-4, not 6-7
- After year 4-5, probability actually decreases as employers assume "something is wrong"

**Cumulative probability over 6 years**: 
- Formula: 1 - (1 - p₁)(1 - p₂)(1 - p₃)(1 - p₄)(1 - p₅)(1 - p₆)

## The Bottleneck Question: Can You Overcome It By Passing More Exams?

### Short Answer: **No, not really.**

### Long Answer with Numbers:

**The Internship Weight in Hiring Decisions**:
- Internship experience: **50-60%** of hiring decision
- Exam progress: **25-30%** of hiring decision  
- Technical skills/projects: **10-15%** of hiring decision
- GPA/school reputation: **5-10%** of hiring decision

**What You're Trying to Do**:
- Boost your 25-30% (exams) from "weak" (1 exam) to "strong" (4-5 exams)
- Improvement: +20-25 percentage points in that category
- Overall hiring score improvement: 20-25% × 0.28 = **+5-7% improvement**

**The Problem**:
- You still have **0% in the 50-60% internship category**
- A candidate with 2 exams + internship scores: (50% + 15% + 8% + 7%) = 80%
- You with 5 exams + no internship score: (0% + 28% + 12% + 7%) = 47%
- **You're still losing by 33 percentage points**

**The Math is Brutal**:
- To overcome internship gap, you'd need to be in 90th+ percentile in ALL other categories
- That means: 6+ exams, perfect GPA, multiple projects, exceptional technical skills
- Even then, you're "interesting" not "preferred"
- Hiring managers think: "Great exams, but why couldn't they get an internship? Red flag."

### Exceptions Where Exams Can Help:
1. **Small regional firms desperate for exam progress** (5-10% of market)
2. **Career changers from related fields** (but you're not one - you're a new grad)
3. **Internal promotions** (but you need to be inside first)

**Bottom Line**: Exams are **necessary but not sufficient**. They cannot fully compensate for zero internship experience in 85-90% of entry-level positions.
