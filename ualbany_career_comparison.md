# Career Choice Analysis: Actuary vs. Software Engineer (UAlbany Student Context)

## 1. The Core Trade-Off (No Metaphors)

You are choosing between **High Barrier/Guaranteed Stability** (Actuary) and **Lower Barrier/Volatile Upside** (Software Engineer).

| Feature | Actuary | Software Engineer (SWE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entry Barrier** | **Extreme**. Requires passing 2-3 professional exams (300+ hours study each) *before* graduation to be competitive. | **Moderate**. Requires a portfolio of projects, LeetCode practice, and a degree. No legal barrier to entry. |
| **Salary (Entry)** | **$70k - $85k**. Standardized, predictable. | **$75k - $120k+**. Highly variable. Big Tech pays more; local Albany jobs pay less. |
| **Salary (Peak)** | **$200k - $400k**. Reached after ~10 years and attaining Fellowship (FSA/FCAS). | **$150k - $500k+**. Highly dependent on company tier (FAANG vs. non-tech) and location. |
| **Job Security** | **Very High**. Regulated industry. Insurance is mandatory. Layoffs are rare even in recessions. | **Low to Medium**. Tech is cyclical. Layoffs are common. Ageism is a real issue after 40. |
| **Location** | **Restricted**. Hubs in NYC, Hartford (CT), Des Moines, Chicago. Hard to find jobs in small towns. | **Flexible**. Every company needs devs. Remote work is standard. Albany has state gov't tech jobs. |
| **The "Grind"** | **Post-College**. You study 20 hrs/week for 7-10 years *while working* to pass exams. | **Pre-College/Continuous**. You grind projects now. Later, you must constantly learn new frameworks to stay relevant. |

---

## 2. The UAlbany Reality (Step-by-Step)

As a UAlbany student, your environment dictates your strategy.

### Path A: Actuarial Science at UAlbany
*   **Department**: Mathematics & Statistics.
*   **The Immediate Step**: You must take courses that prepare you for **Exam P (Probability)** and **Exam FM (Financial Mathematics)**.
*   **The "Filter"**: If you cannot pass Exam P by your Junior year, you are essentially unhireable as an entry-level actuary.
*   **Recruiting**: UAlbany is close to **NYC** and **Hartford, CT** (the "Insurance Capital of the World").
    *   *Strategy*: You target internships in Hartford (Travelers, Aetna, The Hartford) or NYC (MetLife, NY Life).
*   **Fallout Risk**: If you fail the exams, you graduate with a Math degree. You can pivot to Data Analyst or teacher, but it's a "hard pivot."

### Path B: Computer Science (CEAS) at UAlbany
*   **Department**: College of Engineering and Applied Sciences.
*   **The Immediate Step**: Data Structures (CSI 213) is your most important class. Master it.
*   **The "Filter"**: Can you build a project outside of class? If you only do coursework, you will struggle to get hired.
*   **Recruiting**:
    *   *Local*: NY State Government (ITS), CommerceHub (Latham), GlobalFoundries (Malta).
    *   *NYC*: Commuting distance for interviews. Huge market.
*   **Fallout Risk**: If you struggle with coding, you can pivot to IT support, System Admin, or Project Management. Easier "soft landing" than Actuarial.

## 3. Step-by-Step Decision Algorithm

Follow this logic to decide. Do not skip steps.

**Step 1: The Calculus Test**
*   Are you in the top 10% of your class in Calculus 1 & 2?
    *   **No**: Drop Actuary immediately. You will not pass the upper-level exams (Exam IFM/ATPA, Exam LTAM/FAM). Choose Software Engineering or IT.
    *   **Yes**: Proceed to Step 2.

**Step 2: The "Homework" Tolerance Test**
*   Imagine graduating, working 9-5, coming home, and studying math textbooks for 3 hours every night for the next 7 years.
    *   **Reaction "I would hate my life"**: Choose Software Engineering. SWE requires learning, but it's "building things," not taking standardized tests.
    *   **Reaction "I don't mind if it guarantees me $200k"**: Keep Actuary as an option. Proceed to Step 3.

**Step 3: The Risk Profile Test**
*   Do you want to live in NYC/Hartford/Major City, or do you want to live anywhere?
    *   **Anywhere**: Software Engineering. Actuaries are tethered to HQs.
    *   **Hubs are fine**: Proceed to Step 4.

**Step 4: The Market Reality**
*   **Software Engineering**: The entry-level market is currently flooded. You need to be better than average to get a job *right now*.
*   **Actuarial**: The market is stable, but the barrier (exams) keeps the supply low. If you pass 2 exams, you *will* likely get a job.
*   **Decision**:
    *   If you crave **security** and are **excellent at math tests**: **ACTUARY**.
    *   If you crave **building things**, **high salary potential**, and **geographic freedom**: **SOFTWARE ENGINEER**.
