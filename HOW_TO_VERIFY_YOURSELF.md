## HOW TO DO YOUR OWN RESEARCH

### Method 1: Job Listing Analysis (Current Snapshot)

**STEP 1:** Go to job boards
- Indeed.com
- LinkedIn Jobs
- Glassdoor
- SOA Career Center

**STEP 2:** Search for "entry-level actuary" or "actuarial analyst"

**STEP 3:** Count total listings

**STEP 4:** Analyze requirements
- How many exams required?
- Technical skills mentioned?
- Internship required?

**EXAMPLE (as of Dec 2025):**
- Indeed: ~300 entry-level actuarial jobs listed
- LinkedIn: ~600 entry-level listings
- Total actuarial jobs: ~2,000-2,500 listings

**CALCULATION:**
- 300-600 entry-level ÷ 2,000-2,500 total = 12-30% are entry-level
- BUT: This is current listings, not annual openings
- Jobs stay posted 30-60 days typically
- Estimate: 300 current listings × (365/45 days) = 2,433 annual postings

**LIMITATIONS:**
- Some jobs posted on multiple sites (double-counting)
- Some companies don't post publicly (internal hires)
- Job listing duration varies

### Method 2: SOA/CAS Membership Data

**SOURCE:** Society of Actuaries Annual Reports
**LINK:** https://www.soa.org/about/annual-report/

**What to look for:**
- Total membership numbers by credential level
- ASA vs FSA counts
- New credentials awarded per year

**EXAMPLE DATA:**
- Total SOA members: ~31,000
- FSA credentials: ~16,000
- ASA credentials: ~15,000
- New ASAs per year: ~1,200-1,500

**WHAT THIS TELLS YOU:**
- ~1,200-1,500 people achieve ASA per year
- This took them 3-5 years typically
- Working backwards: ~6,000-7,500 people are actively pursuing exams
- Starting pool is much larger (12,000-15,000 take Exam P annually)

**ATTRITION CALCULATION:**
- 12,000-15,000 start Exam P per year
- 1,200-1,500 achieve ASA per year (3-5 years later)
- Success rate: 8-12.5%

**This confirms: ~90% of exam starters don't reach ASA**
