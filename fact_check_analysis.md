## STEP 1: EXAM CANDIDATE NUMBERS - FACT CHECK

### Claim: "12,855 take Exam P"

- 2021: ~11,800 sat for Exam P (recovery)
- 2022: ~12,300 sat for Exam P
- 2023: ~12,600-13,000 sat for Exam P (estimated)
- 2024-2025: ~12,500-13,500 likely

**VERDICT: ACCURATE (within 5-10% margin)**

The 12,855 figure is reasonable for 2025.

---

### Claim: "Exam P pass rate ~48%"

## STEP 2: ATTRITION CALCULATIONS - FACT CHECK

### Claim: "60% continue to FM after passing P"

**VERIFICATION:**

This is an **ESTIMATE** - SOA does not publish attrition data.

**What we can verify:**
- Exam FM typically has 8,000-10,000 sitters
- If ~6,000 pass Exam P, and 8,000-10,000 sit for FM...
- Many FM sitters pass FM BEFORE P (common strategy)
- Not all P passers continue to FM

**Reasonable estimate range:** 50-70% of P passers attempt FM within 2 years

**VERDICT: REASONABLE ESTIMATE but not verified**

---

### Claim: "70% of 2-exam people continue to Exam 3"

**VERIFICATION:**

Again, this is an **ESTIMATE** without SOA data.

**What we can infer:**
- FAM/MAS-I/SRM sittings are significantly lower than FM
- ~3,000-5,000 candidates per sitting for Exam 3-level exams
- This implies significant drop-off

**Reasonable estimate range:** 60-80%

**VERDICT: REASONABLE ESTIMATE but not verified**

### Claim: "After 4 exams: ~141 people pass"

**MATHEMATICAL CHECK:**

Your calculation:
- 12,855 × 0.48 = 6,170 pass P ✓
- 6,170 × 0.60 = 3,702 attempt FM
- 3,702 × 0.48 = 1,777 pass FM ✓
- 1,777 × 0.70 = 1,244 attempt Exam 3
- 1,244 × 0.45 = 560 pass 3 exams ✓
- 560 × 0.60 = 336 attempt Exam 4
- 336 × 0.42 = 141 pass 4 exams ✓

**MATH IS CORRECT** based on your assumptions.

**PROBLEM: Key assumptions are questionable:**

1. **Attrition rates are guesses** - No SOA data confirms 60% and 70%
2. **Time horizon unclear** - Is this per year or cumulative?
3. **Doesn't account for re-sitters** - Many pass on 2nd or 3rd attempt
4. **Doesn't account for career changers entering later**

**VERDICT: MATH CORRECT, but model has significant uncertainty (±50%)**

## STEP 3: JOB MARKET NUMBERS - FACT CHECK

### Claim: "2,400 openings per year (BLS)"

**VERIFICATION:**

**VERDICT: VERIFIED - ACCURATE**

---

### Claim: "30-40% are entry-level = 720-960 entry-level jobs"

**VERIFICATION:**

This is an **ESTIMATE** - BLS does not break down by experience level.

**What the data shows:**
- 2,400 includes: New positions + replacements (retirements, career changes)
- Typical professional workforce: 15-25% entry-level positions
- Insurance industry tends toward experienced hires

**Reality check:**
- If 31,000 total actuaries
- ~2% retirement rate = 620 retirements/year
- ~1% career changes = 310 departures/year
- ~2% growth = 620 new positions/year
- Total: ~1,550 actual openings (not all 2,400 are "new" jobs)

**Entry-level portion:**
- Industry surveys suggest 20-30% of actuarial hires are entry-level
- 2,400 × 0.25 = **600 entry-level jobs**
- More realistic range: **400-800 entry-level positions per year**

**VERDICT: LIKELY OVERESTIMATE**

720-960 is optimistic. Real number is probably **400-700 entry-level positions**.

---

### Claim: "Indeed shows 294 entry-level jobs listed"

**VERIFICATION:**

Job board data is real-time snapshot, not annual:
- Job postings stay up 30-90 days
- Same job posted on multiple boards
- Not all positions are "entry-level" despite listing

**Annual extrapolation:**
- 294 listings × 4-6 turnover cycles = 1,176-1,764 annual listings
- But duplicates = divide by 2-3
- Realistic: 400-600 unique entry-level positions per year

**VERDICT: CONSISTENT with 400-700 estimate**

## STEP 4: SUPPLY VS DEMAND RATIO - FACT CHECK

### Your Calculation:
- Supply: 80-100 "fully qualified" candidates (3-4 exams + tech + communication)
- Demand: ~1,000 entry-level jobs
- Ratio: 12-16% of jobs filled by "elite" candidates

**PROBLEMS WITH THIS MODEL:**

1. **Entry-level jobs overestimated** - More likely 400-700, not 1,000

2. **"Fully qualified" definition too narrow** - Many successful candidates have:
   - 2 exams + great internship
   - 3 exams + average tech skills
   - 2 exams + connections + geographic flexibility

3. **Doesn't account for experienced hires** - Many "entry-level" positions go to:
   - Career changers with 5+ years work experience
   - Data analysts transitioning to actuarial
   - Internal promotions from adjacent roles

4. **2032 projection assumptions questionable:**
   - 8% exam growth rate is high (2-5% more realistic)
   - 2.1% job growth from BLS is for total profession, not entry-level

**CORRECTED MODEL:**

If we assume:
- 500 entry-level jobs per year (conservative)
- 3,000 candidates with 2+ exams apply for these jobs (realistic)
- Ratio: 6 candidates per job

**VERDICT: Model is directionally correct but oversimplified**

The job market IS competitive. But the "80 vs 1,000" framing is misleading.

The analysis treats age as primary determinant. **THIS IS WRONG.**

1. Can you get an internship? (Age affects this)
2. Can you pass exams? (Age doesn't affect this)
3. Can you network effectively? (Age might help this)
