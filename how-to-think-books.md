# Historical "How to Think" Books - Proven by Time

## Timeless Books on Systematic Thinking

### 1. **"How to Solve It" by George Pólya (1945)**
- **Focus**: Systematic problem-solving methodology
- **Approach**: Step-by-step heuristic process
- **Method**: Four-step process (Understand, Plan, Execute, Review)
- **Why Timeless**: Provides concrete, repeatable framework for tackling any problem
- **Status**: Still used in mathematics, engineering, and computer science education

### 2. **"Discourse on Method" by René Descartes (1637)**
- **Focus**: Systematic doubt and logical reasoning
- **Approach**: Four rules for clear thinking
- **Method**: Break problems into parts, proceed from simple to complex
- **Why Timeless**: Foundation of modern scientific method
- **Status**: Influential but more philosophical than practical

### 3. **"Elements" by Euclid (~300 BCE)**
- **Focus**: Logical deduction and proof
- **Approach**: Axioms → Theorems → Proofs
- **Method**: Build from first principles, logical progression
- **Why Timeless**: Foundation of mathematical reasoning
- **Status**: Still taught today, but very domain-specific

### 4. **"The Art of War" by Sun Tzu (~500 BCE)**
- **Focus**: Strategic thinking and decision-making
- **Approach**: Analysis of situations, timing, resources
- **Method**: Assess conditions, adapt strategy
- **Why Timeless**: Principles apply to competition, negotiation, planning
- **Status**: Widely used but metaphorical in places

### 5. **"On the Origin of Species" by Charles Darwin (1859)**
- **Focus**: Systematic observation, hypothesis, evidence
- **Approach**: Observation → Pattern recognition → Theory → Testing
- **Method**: Empirical reasoning, evidence-based conclusions
- **Why Timeless**: Exemplifies scientific thinking process
- **Status**: More about biology than general thinking methods

## **Selected Book: "How to Solve It" by George Pólya**

### Why This Book is True for Today

**Most Relevant Choice**: "How to Solve It" by George Pólya

**Reasons:**

1. **Step-by-Step Process** (Not Metaphorical)
   - Provides exact four-step methodology:
     - Understand the problem
     - Devise a plan
     - Carry out the plan
     - Look back/Review

2. **Practical Heuristics**
   - Lists specific questions to ask:
     - "What is the unknown?"
     - "What are the data?"
     - "What is the condition?"
     - "Have you seen this before?"
     - "Can you use a similar problem?"

3. **Tested by Time**
   - Published 1945, still in print
   - Used in mathematics, computer science, engineering curricula worldwide
   - Translated into multiple languages
   - Cited in thousands of academic papers

4. **Directly Applicable Today**
   - Used in software development (debugging, algorithm design)
   - Applied in engineering problem-solving
   - Taught in business schools for decision-making
   - Framework works for any domain: technical, business, personal

5. **No Metaphor or Abstraction**
   - Concrete questions and steps
   - Specific techniques (working backwards, considering special cases)
   - Actionable checklist format
   - Examples from real problems

### The Four-Step Method (From Pólya)

**Step 1: Understand the Problem**
- What is unknown? What are the data?
- What is the condition?
- Is the condition sufficient? Is it redundant or contradictory?
- Draw a figure, introduce suitable notation

**Step 2: Devise a Plan**
- Have you seen this problem before?
- Can you use a similar problem?
- Can you restate the problem?
- Can you solve part of the problem?
- Can you work backwards?

**Step 3: Carry Out the Plan**
- Execute each step
- Check each step
- Can you prove each step is correct?

**Step 4: Look Back**
- Check the result
- Can you check the reasoning?
- Can you use the result for another problem?
- Can you solve it differently?

### Why This Over Others

- **Descartes**: Too philosophical, abstract methods
- **Euclid**: Domain-specific (geometry), requires mathematical background
- **Sun Tzu**: Metaphorical, strategic rather than problem-solving
- **Darwin**: Example of scientific thinking, not a how-to guide

**Pólya's book** gives you a concrete toolkit you can use immediately, regardless of your field or background.
