# Actuary vs Software Engineer: Step-by-Step Decision Guide for UAlbany Student

## Your Starting Point

**University at Albany, NY**
- UAlbany does NOT have a dedicated actuarial science program
- UAlbany DOES have Computer Science and Mathematics programs
- Nearest actuarial program: SUNY Albany's Mathematics department offers actuarial courses but limited
- This affects your preparation path significantly

---

## Step 1: What Each Career Actually Requires

### Software Engineer Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, Software Engineering, or related field
- UAlbany offers BS in Computer Science - directly applicable
- No mandatory certifications after graduation
- No exams required to practice

**Skills to learn:**
- Programming languages (Python, Java, JavaScript, C++)
- Data structures and algorithms
- Databases and SQL
- Web development or mobile development
- Version control (Git)
- Software design patterns

**Time to job-ready:** 4 years (undergraduate degree)

### Actuary Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree in Mathematics, Statistics, Actuarial Science, or related field
- UAlbany offers Mathematics degree - applicable but not specialized
- VEE (Validation by Educational Experience) courses required

**Mandatory Exams (SOA - Society of Actuaries path):**
- Exam P (Probability) - 3 hours, $275
- Exam FM (Financial Mathematics) - 3 hours, $275
- Exam FAM (Fundamentals of Actuarial Mathematics) - 3 hours 30 min, $350
- Exam ALTAM or ASTAM (Advanced Topics) - $350
- Plus additional exams for ASA/FSA designation

**Time to job-ready:** 
- Entry-level position: 4 years degree + 2-3 exams passed = 4-5 years
- Associate (ASA): 7-10 years total
- Fellow (FSA): 10-15 years total

## Step 2: Job Market Numbers (2024-2025 Data)

### Software Engineer Job Market

**Total U.S. job openings (annual):**
- Approximately 162,000 new positions per year
- 1.5 million total employed software developers in U.S.

**New York State specifically:**
- ~15,000 software engineering jobs posted annually
- Concentrated in: NYC, Albany Capital Region, Long Island
- Remote work common (expands options significantly)

**Entry-level competition:**
- Applications per job: 100-300 for entry-level
- Hiring rate: 1-3% of applicants
- Internship conversion: 50-70% at good companies

### Actuary Job Market

**Total U.S. job openings (annual):**
- Approximately 2,500 new positions per year
- 28,000 total employed actuaries in U.S.

**New York State specifically:**
- ~400 actuarial jobs posted annually
- Concentrated in: NYC (insurance companies), Hartford CT (nearby)
- Remote work less common for entry-level

**Entry-level competition:**
- Applications per job: 50-150 for entry-level
- Requires 2-3 exams passed before most will interview
- Internship extremely important (85% of hires have internship)

### Direct Comparison

**Key fact:** Software engineering has 65x more job openings than actuarial.

## Step 4: Day-to-Day Work Reality

### Software Engineer Daily Work

**Typical day:**
- 2-4 hours: Writing code
- 1-2 hours: Meetings (stand-ups, planning, reviews)
- 1-2 hours: Code review (your code and others')
- 1-2 hours: Debugging, testing
- 1 hour: Documentation, communication

**Work environment:**
- Usually open office or remote
- Flexible hours common
- Dress code: Casual (t-shirts, jeans)
- Team size: 5-15 people typically

**What you actually do:**
- Build features users interact with
- Fix bugs in production systems
- Maintain existing codebases
- Write tests and documentation
- Collaborate with product managers, designers

**Downsides:**
- On-call rotations (nights/weekends for some roles)
- Tight deadlines during releases
- Constant learning required (technologies change fast)
- Age discrimination exists (harder after 50)
- Layoffs more common in downturns

### Actuary Daily Work

**Typical day:**
- 3-4 hours: Excel/Python modeling
- 2-3 hours: Data analysis and validation
- 1-2 hours: Meetings with business stakeholders
- 1-2 hours: Report writing, documentation
- During exam periods: 2-3 hours evening studying

**Work environment:**
- Office-based (more traditional)
- 9-5 more common than tech
- Dress code: Business casual
- Team size: 3-10 people typically

**What you actually do:**
- Calculate insurance reserves
- Price insurance products
- Model risk scenarios
- Prepare regulatory filings
- Analyze claims data

**Downsides:**
- Exams consume your 20s (15-20 hours/week studying for 7-10 years)
- Work can be repetitive (reconciling numbers)
- Less variety than tech
- Insurance industry moves slowly
- Geographic constraints (must be near insurance hubs)

## Step 7: Risk Assessment

### Software Engineer Risks

**High probability risks:**
1. **Layoffs during economic downturns** (30-40% chance in career)
   - Tech layoffs 2022-2023 affected 300,000+ workers
   - Recovery time: 3-6 months typically
   
2. **Skills becoming obsolete** (100% certainty)
   - Must continuously learn new technologies
   - What you learn in college will be outdated in 5-10 years

3. **Age discrimination** (after age 45-50)
   - Harder to get hired at older ages
   - Must move to management or stay cutting-edge

**Lower probability risks:**
- Company goes bankrupt (startup risk)
- Outsourcing to overseas (reduced but exists)
- AI replacing coding jobs (emerging concern)

### Actuary Risks

**High probability risks:**
1. **Failing exams** (40-60% don't complete all exams)
   - Each exam has 40-50% pass rate
   - Failing multiple times can stall career
   
2. **Not getting first job** (35% of exam-passers struggle)
   - Without internship, very difficult
   - Geographic limitations

3. **Automation reducing positions** (ongoing)
   - Entry-level work being automated
   - Fewer positions being created

**Lower probability risks:**
- Insurance industry disruption
- Credential devaluation
- Offshoring of actuarial work

### Risk Comparison

| Risk Type | Software Engineer | Actuary |
|-----------|------------------|---------|
| Job loss probability | Higher (layoffs common) | Lower (stable industry) |
| Recovery from job loss | Faster (more jobs) | Slower (fewer jobs) |
| Career failure risk | Lower (no exams to fail) | Higher (exam failure) |
| Long-term stability | Lower (ageism, tech changes) | Higher (career viable to 65) |
| Geographic flexibility | Higher | Lower |

## Step 9: UAlbany-Specific Action Plan

### If You Choose Software Engineering

**Freshman year:**
1. Declare Computer Science major
2. Take CS 101, CS 201 (intro programming)
3. Learn Python on your own (Codecademy, free)
4. Build 1 simple project (personal website, simple game)

**Sophomore year:**
1. Take data structures, algorithms courses
2. Learn Git/GitHub
3. Start applying to summer internships (December-February)
4. Build 2-3 projects for portfolio

**Junior year:**
1. Complete internship (summer)
2. Take software engineering electives
3. Contribute to open source or build significant project
4. Apply to more internships or convert to full-time offer

**Senior year:**
1. Apply to 50-100+ jobs (start September)
2. Practice LeetCode for coding interviews (200+ problems)
3. Prepare system design knowledge
4. Graduate with job offer or continue search

### If You Choose Actuary

**Freshman year:**
1. Declare Mathematics major
2. Take Calculus I, II, III
3. Start studying for Exam P (Probability)
4. Join Gamma Iota Sigma (insurance/actuarial fraternity) if available, or start one

**Sophomore year:**
1. Pass Exam P (aim for spring)
2. Take probability and statistics courses
3. Start studying for Exam FM
4. Learn Excel advanced functions

**Junior year:**
1. Pass Exam FM (aim for fall)
2. Take financial mathematics courses
3. Apply to actuarial internships (December-January)
4. Start learning Python/R for actuarial work

**Senior year:**
1. Complete internship (summer before senior year)
2. Pass one more exam (FAM or STAM)
3. Apply to 50+ actuarial analyst positions
4. Complete VEE requirements

## Final Recommendation

### For a UAlbany student specifically:

**Software Engineering is the more practical choice because:**

1. **UAlbany's CS program directly prepares you** - no program adaptation needed
2. **More job opportunities** - 65x more openings nationally
3. **Geographic flexibility** - can stay in Albany or work remote
4. **No exam burden** - your 20s aren't consumed by studying
5. **Higher first-decade earnings** - $100,000+ gap over 10 years
6. **Lower risk of career failure** - no exams to fail repeatedly

**Actuary is still viable if:**
- You score in top 5% on math tests naturally
- You're certain you can study 15-20 hours/week for 10 years
- You're willing to relocate to Hartford, NYC, or other insurance hub
- You strongly prefer stability over flexibility
- You've already passed Exam P or know you can

### The Test

**Do this before deciding:**

1. Take a practice Exam P (free online)
2. Complete 1 coding project (make a simple app)
3. See which one you enjoy more
4. See which one you're better at

If math comes naturally and you enjoy the exam prep: Consider actuary
If building things excites you and coding clicks: Go software engineering
