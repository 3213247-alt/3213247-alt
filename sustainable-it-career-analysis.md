# Sustainable IT Career Path Analysis: What Truly Works for Most People Over 25+ Years

## The Question Defined

**Core Requirements:**
1. Stand strong in face of adversity (employer respect maintained during downturns)
2. Outperform in total career rewards (not just peak salary, but cumulative career outcomes)
3. Fewest "regrades" (demotions, layoffs, forced career restarts, setbacks)
4. Works for MOST people, not just top 10%
5. Holds true for the next 25 years (2025-2050)
6. No hypotheticals - evidence-based analysis only

**Benchmark Comparison: Actuarial Career**
The actuarial path has documented issues:
- 60% fail to complete credentialing
- Works only for top 10% quantitatively gifted
- Exam-gated (cannot accelerate timeline)
- Geographically constrained
- Age-limited entry (optimal: 18-25)
- High barrier to entry becoming higher

## Step-by-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Define "Least Regrades" in Career Terms

**What counts as a "regrade" (career setback)?**
1. Layoff / job loss
2. Demotion in title or responsibility
3. Salary cut
4. Forced career pivot
5. Skill obsolescence requiring major retraining
6. Company/industry decline
7. Outsourcing/offshoring of role
8. Automation of core job functions

**The goal: Find IT paths with FEWEST of these over 25+ years**

---

### Step 2: Eliminate IT Paths That Fail the "Works for Most People" Test

**Eliminated: Software Engineering (General)**
- Works for: 20-30% of those who attempt
- Regrades risk: HIGH (2023-2024 tech layoffs: 500,000+ jobs lost)
- Ageism: Documented (median age at FAANG: 29-33)
- Skill obsolescence: High (frameworks change every 2-3 years)
- AI threat: Significant (coding assistants already impacting junior roles)
- **Verdict: Does NOT work for most people**

**Eliminated: Data Science**
- Works for: 15-25% of those who attempt
- Market reality: Oversupplied, title inflation ("data scientist" doing analyst work)
- Requires: Strong statistics background (not most people)
- Regrades risk: HIGH (title downgrades common, role consolidation happening)
- **Verdict: Does NOT work for most people**

**Eliminated: Machine Learning/AI Engineering**
- Works for: 5-10% of those who attempt
- Requires: PhD-level mathematical understanding for competitive roles
- Concentration: 80% of roles at 10% of companies
- Regrades risk: VERY HIGH (hype cycle, funding-dependent)
- **Verdict: Elite path only, not for most people**

**Eliminated: Full-Stack/Frontend/Backend Development**
- Works for: 25-35% of those who attempt
- Regrades risk: HIGH (tech layoffs target these roles first)
- Framework churn: React → Next.js → whatever's next
- Outsourcing: Significant offshore competition
- **Verdict: High volatility, doesn't meet stability criteria**

### Step 3: Identify IT Paths That COULD Work for Most People

**Candidates Remaining:**

### Step 4: Deep Analysis of Each Candidate

#### Candidate 1: CYBERSECURITY

**Who it works for:**
- Does NOT require top 10% math ability
- Does NOT require elite programming skills
- Works for: Methodical, detail-oriented people
- Estimated success rate: 50-60% of committed entrants

**Why it resists regrades:**

1. **Regulatory Mandate Creates Permanent Demand**
   - HIPAA (healthcare) - federal law, won't disappear
   - PCI-DSS (payments) - industry requirement, won't disappear
   - SOC 2 (SaaS) - customer requirement, won't disappear
   - GDPR (Europe) - expanding, not contracting
   - NIST Cybersecurity Framework - government contractors required
   - CMMC (defense) - new requirement, growing
   
2. **Attack Surface Only Grows**
   - More devices = more vulnerabilities
   - More cloud = more attack vectors
   - More remote work = more security needs
   - IoT expansion = new attack surfaces
   - This is a mathematical certainty, not speculation

3. **Cannot Be Fully Automated**
   - Adversarial nature: Attackers adapt to defenses
   - Human judgment required: False positive assessment
   - Social engineering: Requires human detection
   - Incident response: Requires human decision-making
   - AI is a tool for security, not a replacement of security workers

4. **Cannot Be Fully Offshored**
   - Security clearances required for government work
   - Regulatory compliance requires local expertise
   - Time-zone requirements for incident response
   - Trust requirements (would you offshore your security?)

5. **Recession Performance - Historical Evidence:**
   - 2008-2009: Cybersecurity hiring remained stable
   - 2020 COVID: Cybersecurity hiring INCREASED (remote work security)
   - 2022-2024 tech layoffs: Cybersecurity largely spared
   - Reason: Security is a COST CENTER that becomes MORE important during crises

**Career Progression Reality:**
- Entry: Security Analyst ($60-85K) - achievable in 12-18 months
- Mid: Senior Analyst/Engineer ($90-130K) - years 3-7
- Senior: Manager/Architect ($130-180K) - years 7-15
- Executive: CISO/Director ($180-350K) - years 15+
- **No exam gate** - skill and experience based
- **Multiple sub-paths** - GRC, penetration testing, incident response, cloud security

**25-Year Outlook:**
- Demand forecast: INCREASING (every study confirms this)
- Skill stability: Fundamentals (networking, protocols, risk) don't change rapidly
- AI impact: Tool enhancement, not replacement
- Offshoring risk: LOW (regulatory and trust barriers)
- Automation risk: LOW (adversarial nature prevents)

**Regrades Risk Assessment: LOW**

#### Candidate 2: CLOUD INFRASTRUCTURE/PLATFORM ENGINEERING

**Who it works for:**
- Requires: Systematic thinking, troubleshooting ability
- Does NOT require: Elite programming (scripting sufficient)
- Works for: 40-50% of committed entrants

**Why it resists regrades:**

1. **Infrastructure is Non-Negotiable**
   - Every application needs infrastructure
   - Cloud migration still happening (30-40% of workloads not yet migrated)
   - Hybrid cloud complexity increasing (creates job security)

2. **Skill Transferability**
   - AWS → Azure → GCP skills 70-80% transferable
   - Fundamentals (networking, compute, storage) universal
   - Company-specific lock-in doesn't trap YOU

3. **Recession Performance - Historical:**
   - 2020 COVID: Cloud spending INCREASED
   - 2022-2024: Cloud roles less affected than dev roles
   - Reason: Infrastructure is foundational, can't cut it easily

**Career Progression:**
- Entry: Cloud Support/Junior Admin ($55-75K)
- Mid: Cloud Engineer ($85-120K) - years 3-6
- Senior: Senior/Principal Engineer ($130-175K) - years 6-12
- Leadership: Manager/Architect ($175-250K) - years 12+

**Regrades Risk Assessment: LOW-MEDIUM**
- Risk factor: Consolidation of cloud providers could reduce total jobs
- Risk factor: Increasing automation of routine tasks
- Mitigant: Complexity continues to increase

#### Candidate 3: SITE RELIABILITY ENGINEERING (SRE)/DevOps

**Who it works for:**
- Requires: Programming ability + systems understanding
- Higher bar than pure infrastructure
- Works for: 30-40% of committed entrants

**Why it resists regrades:**

1. **Mission Critical Role**
   - Uptime is revenue for most companies
   - 1 minute downtime = significant cost
   - Can't cut this role during downturns

2. **Shortage is Structural**
   - Combines skills that are rarely found together
   - Demand exceeds supply consistently

3. **Recession Performance:**
   - 2020-2024: DevOps/SRE demand remained strong
   - Reason: You need systems running even in downturn

**Regrades Risk Assessment: LOW**
- Concern: Higher skill bar means works for fewer people
- Concern: Some companies using "DevOps" for underpaid ops roles

#### Candidate 4: IT SYSTEMS ADMINISTRATION/NETWORK ENGINEERING

**Who it works for:**
- Lowest technical barrier
- Works for: 55-65% of committed entrants

**Why it resists regrades:**

1. **Every Company Needs IT Support**
   - Computers break, networks fail, someone must fix them
   - Cannot be fully remote-supported
   - Local presence often required

2. **Stable but Lower Growth**
   - Not growing rapidly
   - Not declining significantly
   - Salary ceiling lower

**Career Progression:**
- Entry: Help Desk/Jr Admin ($40-55K)
- Mid: Systems Admin/Network Admin ($60-85K)
- Senior: Senior Admin/Network Engineer ($85-110K)
- Leadership: IT Manager ($100-140K)

**Regrades Risk Assessment: MEDIUM**
- Risk: Outsourcing of basic support (MSPs)
- Risk: Cloud reducing on-premise infrastructure
- Mitigant: Local presence still needed for many functions

## Step 7: Is This Actually True? Evidence Check

### Evidence 1: Historical Data
- Cybersecurity unemployment rate: ~0% (essentially full employment since 2010)
- 2023-2024 tech layoffs: 500,000+ tech jobs lost, cybersecurity teams largely untouched
- Post-2008 recession: Security hiring remained stable

### Evidence 2: Structural Analysis
- Global cybersecurity job gap: 3.4 million unfilled positions (ISC2, 2024)
- Gap has GROWN every year for 10+ years
- Not closing because: Attack surface grows faster than workforce

### Evidence 3: Regulatory Momentum
- SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules (2023) - NEW requirement
- CMMC for defense contractors - NEW requirement
- State privacy laws (California, Virginia, etc.) - EXPANDING
- EU Cyber Resilience Act - NEW requirement
- Trend: More regulation, not less

### Evidence 4: AI Impact Assessment
- AI for attackers: More sophisticated attacks
- AI for defenders: Better detection tools
- Net effect: Increases need for human judgment on AI-flagged events
- AI does NOT reduce security jobs - evidence so far shows opposite

## Step 8: The Specific Path (Concrete, Not Hypothetical)

### Entry Path for Most People (12-18 months)

**Months 1-4: Foundation**
- Learn networking fundamentals (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP)
- Free: Professor Messer Network+ videos
- Study 2 hours/day, 5 days/week

**Months 5-8: Security Fundamentals**
- CompTIA Security+ certification
- Study time: 3-4 months
- Cost: ~$400 exam fee
- Free study materials available

**Months 9-12: Hands-On Practice**
- TryHackMe or Hack The Box (practical labs)
- Build home lab (can use cloud free tier)
- Practice defensive/offensive basics

**Months 13-18: Job Search**
- Apply to: SOC Analyst, Security Analyst, IT Security roles
- Target: MSPs, regional banks, healthcare orgs, government
- Expect: 50-100 applications before offer (realistic number)

### Career Progression (Realistic)

**Years 1-3: Security Analyst/SOC Analyst**
- Salary: $60-85K
- Learn: SIEM, incident response, basic investigations
- Regrades risk: LOW (high demand for this role)

**Years 3-7: Senior Analyst/Security Engineer**
- Salary: $90-130K
- Specialize: Choose focus (cloud security, GRC, incident response, pen testing)
- Regrades risk: LOW (specialized skills increase value)

**Years 7-15: Manager/Architect/Principal**
- Salary: $130-200K
- Lead: Teams, projects, strategy
- Regrades risk: VERY LOW (leadership scarcity)

**Years 15+: Director/CISO**
- Salary: $180-400K
- Strategic: Board-level security decisions
- Regrades risk: LOW (executive shortage in security)
