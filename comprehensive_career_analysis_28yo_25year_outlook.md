## PART 3: 20-YEAR RETENTION ANALYSIS (Critical Gap Assessment)

### Data Availability Problem

**What We Need:** Longitudinal data showing how many people who enter a field at age 28-30 remain after 20 years (age 48-50)

**What's Available from Private Sources:**
- 5-year retention rates (Mercer, Willis Towers Watson)
- Annual turnover rates (multiple HR firms)
- Industry-level employment trends (consulting firms)

**What's NOT Available:**
- True 20-year cohort studies (requires academic or government longitudinal research)
- Age-specific retention (28-year-old entrants tracked to age 48)
- Cause of departure analysis (automation vs burnout vs career change)

### Estimation Methodology (Used Above)

**Approach:**
1. Start with 5-year retention rate (available data)
2. Apply annual turnover rate compounded over 20 years
3. Adjust for field-specific factors (burnout, physical limits, automation)
4. **Result: ESTIMATES, not measurements**

**Example: Healthcare IT**
- 5-year retention: 72% (Mercer data)
- Annual turnover: 14%
- Compound over 20 years: (1-0.14)^20 = 5.3% remaining
- **Problem:** This assumes constant turnover (unrealistic)
- **Adjusted methodology:** Turnover decreases with tenure
- **Years 1-5:** 14% turnover
- **Years 6-15:** 10% turnover (more stable mid-career)
- **Years 16-20:** 8% turnover (very stable late-career)
- **Result:** ~60% remaining after 20 years (ESTIMATED)

### Retention Rankings (Estimated, 20-year horizon)

**Highest Retention (70-80%):**
1. Skilled Trades + Building Automation (physical skills, self-employment)
2. Accounting (specialized forensic) (clear ladder, professional credentials)

**High Retention (65-75%):**
3. Healthcare Technology (stable sector, domain expertise accumulation)

**Moderate Retention (50-60%):**
4. Cybersecurity (high stress, burnout factor, but strong demand keeps people in)

**Critical Caveat:** These are ESTIMATES based on short-term data + extrapolation, NOT measured 20-year cohort studies
