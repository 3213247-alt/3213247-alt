# Verification Results: Actuarial Career Analysis Claims

## Executive Summary

**Overall Assessment:** The analysis contains a mix of:
- **Reasonably accurate general trends** (exam difficulty, competition, importance of internships)
- **Unsupported specific numbers** (12,855 Exam P takers, precise percentages)
- **Questionable assumptions** (attrition rates, filtering percentages)
- **Unverified claims** (age discrimination extent, career changer success rates)
- **Mathematical errors** in some calculations

**Accuracy Level:** Approximately **40-50% verifiable**, **30-40% reasonable estimates**, **20-30% unsupported assumptions**

---

## DETAILED VERIFICATION BY CLAIM

### CLAIM 1: 12,855 Exam P Takers in 2025

**Status: UNVERIFIED - NO SOURCE PROVIDED**

**What We Know:**
- SOA does publish exam statistics, but specific 2025 projections not cited
- Historical data shows Exam P typically has 5,000-15,000 candidates per year globally
- Number could be plausible but needs verification

**Verdict:** **ASSUMPTION** - Cannot verify without source

---

### CLAIM 2: Exam Pass Rates

**Status: PARTIALLY ACCURATE**

**What We Know from SOA Data:**
- Exam P pass rates: Typically **40-50%** (varies by sitting)
- Exam FM pass rates: Typically **40-50%** (varies by sitting)
- Advanced exams (FAM, MAS-I, etc.): Typically **35-50%** pass rates
- Pass rates fluctuate significantly by exam sitting

**Verdict:** **GENERALLY ACCURATE** - Pass rates are in the claimed range, though specific percentages vary

**Issue:** Claim uses single percentages (48%, 45%) when pass rates vary by 10-15 percentage points between sittings

---

- 60% continue from Exam P to Exam FM

### CLAIM 11: Final Calculations and Ratios

**Status: MATH IS CORRECT, BUT PREMISES ARE QUESTIONABLE**

**Calculations:**
- 150-185 candidates ÷ 1,157 jobs = 12-16% ✓ **MATH CORRECT**
- But this assumes:
  - Only these candidates can fill jobs (FALSE - jobs go to many types of candidates)
  - Jobs are filled sequentially (FALSE - multiple candidates compete per job)
  - All qualified candidates apply to all jobs (FALSE - geographic/company preferences)

**Verdict:** **MATH CORRECT, LOGIC FLAWED**

**Issue:** The ratio calculation doesn't mean what the analysis claims. It's not "12-16% of jobs filled by qualified candidates" - it's "qualified candidates represent 12-16% of job openings" which is different.

---

## CRITICAL LOGICAL FLAWS IDENTIFIED

### Flaw 1: The "Fully Qualified" Assumption
**Problem:** Analysis assumes only candidates with 3-4 exams + tech + communication can get jobs.

**Reality:** 
- Many entry-level jobs go to candidates with 1-2 exams + internship
- Some go to candidates with strong internships but fewer exams
- Geographic flexibility, networking, and timing matter enormously

**Impact:** Overstates the exclusivity of the "qualified" pool

---

### Flaw 2: Linear Progression Assumption
**Problem:** Analysis assumes linear progression: Exam P → FM → 3 → 4, with fixed attrition rates.

**Reality:**
- Many candidates skip exams or take them out of order
- Some pass exams faster, some slower
- Attrition is not uniform - varies by candidate quality, motivation, circumstances

**Impact:** The funnel calculation may be inaccurate

---

### Flaw 3: Age Discrimination Overstatement
**Problem:** Claims age 34+ is "automatic filter" and reduces success rate to 20-30%.

**Reality:**
- Age discrimination exists but is not absolute
- Many successful career changers in actuarial field
- Age 34 is not protected class (ADEA protects 40+)
- Success depends more on credentials, networking, and persistence than age alone

**Impact:** May discourage qualified older candidates unnecessarily

---

### Flaw 4: Missing Key Factors
**Problem:** Analysis focuses on exams + tech + communication but ignores:
- Geographic flexibility (HUGE factor)
- Networking and connections
- Internship quality and company relationships
- Interview skills and cultural fit
- Timing (when you're in job market)
- Company size and type preferences

**Impact:** Oversimplifies a complex hiring process

---

### Flaw 5: Success Rate Definition Unclear
**Problem:** "Success rate" is never clearly defined:
- Get any actuarial job?
- Get job within 6 months? 12 months? Ever?
- Get job at desired company/compensation level?

**Impact:** Makes success rate claims unverifiable and potentially misleading
