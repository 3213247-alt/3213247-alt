This eliminates:
- Fields where only top 10-20% earn well
- Winner-take-all industries
- Fields with extreme income variance
- Industries where median income ≪ mean income

Identify the career path that satisfies BOTH constraints simultaneously.

## PART 2: ROI ANALYSIS - MOST PEOPLE, NOT JUST TOP PERFORMERS

### Critical Framework: Median vs. Mean Income

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION**: Many fields have HIGH MEAN income but LOW MEDIAN income.

Example:
- Field A: Mean $150K, Median $150K (symmetric distribution - MOST people do well)
- Field B: Mean $150K, Median $75K (skewed distribution - only top do well)

**We need Field A characteristics.**

### Metrics for "Most People" ROI:
1. **Median income** (not mean)
2. **10th-25th percentile income** (floor matters)
3. **Employment rate** (can you actually get a job?)
4. **Income variance** (low = more predictable)
5. **Entry barrier accessibility** (can average person enter?)

- Nursing: 2-3 years without full income
- Electrician: Earns while learning

**5. Personal Preference for Work Type**
- Nursing: Patient interaction, emotional labor

- **Cavity**: Person's work preference not assessed

## PART 6: FINAL THOUGHTS AND SYNTHESIS

### What I Think (Step-by-Step Reasoning):

**Step 1**: The constraint of "ROI for MOST people" eliminates many popular recommendations (tech startups, law, entrepreneurship, creative fields).

**Step 2**: The age 28+ constraint eliminates fields with documented ageism (consumer tech, startups, investment banking entry).

**Step 3**: The 25-year horizon requires fields that cannot be automated or outsourced.

**Step 4**: Cross-referencing these constraints leaves: Healthcare, Skilled Trades, Cybersecurity, Enterprise IT.

**Step 5**: Among these, Nursing and Electrical have the STRONGEST "most people" ROI because:
- Licensed professions create wage floors
- Critical shortages ensure employment
- Physical presence required (cannot be offshored)
- Clear progression paths

**Step 6**: Nursing edges out Electrical because:
- Higher ultimate ceiling (NP pathway)
- Less physical wear on body over 30+ year career
- More geographic flexibility
- Better work environment in later career

**Step 7**: However, Electrical is BETTER if:
- Person prefers physical work
- Person wants to earn while learning
- Person has entrepreneurial inclinations (business ownership)
- Person wants zero student debt

**Step 8**: Cybersecurity is BETTER if:
- Person is technical/analytical
- Person wants desk work
- Person is already in IT-adjacent field
- Person has short financial runway (faster entry)

### Is There Something Critical Missing?

**YES - Three critical elements**:

**1. Healthcare costs during training**
- If person loses current health insurance during training, this is a major cost not included
- Nursing/trade programs often don't include student health insurance

**2. Opportunity cost of current income**
- Analysis assumes person is entering workforce for first time or underemployed
- If person has $80K+ job currently, opportunity cost changes calculation significantly

**3. Interest rates / economic cycle**
- Training financed by debt is affected by interest rates
- Economic recession during training could affect entry timing
- 2025-2030 economic conditions uncertain

### The Bottom Line:

**For a 28-year-old entering the workforce with the goal of maximum ROI for MOST people over 25 years**:

**Primary Recommendation**: **Nursing (RN → NP pathway)**
- Highest combination of age-friendliness + mass ROI + 25-year stability
- Works for average candidate, not just top performers
- Licensed profession protects wages
- Cannot be automated or outsourced
- Clear advancement path

**Alternative if prefer physical work**: **Electrical Trade**
- Near-zero training cost
- Earn while learning
- EV/solar boom ensures 25-year demand
- Business ownership option

**Alternative if prefer technical/desk work**: **Cybersecurity**
- Fastest entry pathway
- High income ceiling
- Remote work available
- Age-neutral hiring
