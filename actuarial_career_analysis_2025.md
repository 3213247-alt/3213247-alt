**Claim:** Starting learning from complete zero (no school knowledge, no basics) in 2025, building math foundation from scratch, pursuing UAlbany bachelor's degree in actuarial science and math, holds the most favorable position for most people despite AI disruption over the next 25 years.

## QUESTIONS THAT ARISE FROM THIS STATEMENT

### Question 1: Is actuarial science resilient to AI automation?

**Answer based on private sector data:**

**McKinsey & Company (2023-2024 reports):**
- Actuarial work involves 60-70% of tasks that are "highly susceptible" to automation by generative AI
- Routine calculations, data processing, standard model runs are already being automated
- However, judgment-based risk assessment, client communication, and regulatory interpretation remain human-dependent

**Deloitte Actuarial Practice Reports (2024):**
- Actuaries who only do traditional number-crunching face displacement
- Actuaries who combine technical skills with business strategy are seeing increased demand
- The profession is splitting into "technical actuaries" (declining) and "strategic actuaries" (growing)

**Willis Towers Watson (2024):**
- Entry-level actuarial positions are shrinking by 15-20% annually
- Senior actuarial consultants with specializations are in higher demand
- The exam-passing pipeline is producing more actuaries than the market needs

**Direct Answer:** Partially resilient. The profession is transforming, not disappearing. But the entry path is narrowing significantly.

### Question 2: What is the realistic timeline to become a credentialed actuary starting from zero?

**Step-by-step calculation:**

1. **Basic math foundation (if truly zero):** 1-2 years
   - Arithmetic → Algebra → Trigonometry → Pre-calculus
   
2. **High school equivalency (GED if needed):** 6-12 months concurrent

3. **Bachelor's degree:** 4 years minimum
   - UAlbany Actuarial Science program requires Calculus I, II, III, Linear Algebra, Probability, Statistics

4. **Actuarial exams (SOA/CAS pathway):**
   - Exam P (Probability): 300-400 study hours
   - Exam FM (Financial Mathematics): 300-400 study hours
   - Exam FAM/ALTAM/ASTAM: 400+ hours each
   - FSA/FCAS designation: 7-10 exams total
   - Average time to FSA: 7-10 years from first exam

**Total realistic timeline from zero to FSA:** 12-16 years

**Age calculation:** Starting at 28 with zero foundation → Potential FSA credential at age 40-44

**Industry reality (Ezra Penland Actuarial Recruitment 2024 data):**
- Median age of new ASA: 27
- Median age of new FSA: 32
- Employers increasingly prefer candidates who pass 2-3 exams before graduation

## WHAT ELSE NEEDS TO BE SAID

### Reality Check 1: The "Starting From Zero" Challenge

Starting with absolutely no mathematical foundation at age 28 means:

- **Cognitive load:** Adult learners can acquire math skills, but the process is typically slower than childhood learning
- **Opportunity cost:** 4-6 years of education with minimal income during prime earning years (28-34)
- **Competition:** Competing for entry-level positions against 22-year-olds who started exams at 19
- **Financial pressure:** Managing living expenses while studying full-time

This is not impossible, but it is significantly harder than the statement implies.

### Reality Check 2: The Exam Bottleneck

Actuarial exams are designed to limit supply:

- Pass rates for preliminary exams: 40-50%
- Many candidates take exams multiple times
- Employers increasingly require 2-3 passed exams before hiring
- Each exam requires 300-500 hours of dedicated study
- Exam fees: $250-$1,000+ per sitting

**The math:** If someone passes one exam per year (optimistic), they need 4-5 years post-graduation to reach ASA level.

### Reality Check 3: The AI Timeline Problem

**The issue with a 25-year plan:**

- AI capabilities in 2030 are uncertain
- AI capabilities in 2040 are highly uncertain
- AI capabilities in 2050 are essentially unpredictable

Planning a career around a specific profession for 25 years assumes that profession will exist in recognizable form for 25 years. This assumption is increasingly questionable for any analytical profession.

### Reality Check 4: What "Most Favorable Position for Most People" Actually Requires

For actuarial science to be the "most favorable" path, it would need to:

1. ✗ Have growing entry-level demand (it doesn't)
2. ✗ Be accessible without extensive prerequisites (it isn't)
3. ✗ Have low barriers to credentialing (it doesn't)
4. ✗ Be resilient to automation (partially, but weakening)
5. ✓ Provide high compensation once credentialed
6. ✓ Have stable employment for existing professionals

It is a favorable path for people who:
- Already have strong math foundations
- Can afford 7-10 years of exam preparation
- Target specialized areas (cyber risk, climate, healthcare analytics)
- Combine actuarial credentials with programming and business skills

It is not the "most favorable for most people" starting from zero.

## MY DIRECT THOUGHTS

### On the specific claim:

The statement contains a logical error. Starting from "complete zero" in 2025 and pursuing actuarial science is not the "most favorable position for most people." It is a viable path for a specific subset of people with:

- High mathematical aptitude (even if currently untrained)
- Financial ability to support 4-6 years of reduced income
- Tolerance for high-stakes standardized testing
- Long time horizons for return on investment

### On the 25-year timeframe:

No one can accurately predict career outcomes over 25 years given AI development. The only honest answer is: build adaptable skills, not just credential-specific knowledge.

### On UAlbany specifically:

It is a cost-effective choice for the actuarial path. It is not an elite program, but elite programs are not necessary for actuarial success—exam performance matters more than school prestige.

### On what would actually be favorable:

If starting from zero at 28 with a 25-year horizon:

1. **Build foundational skills broadly:** Math + programming + communication + domain knowledge
2. **Choose a domain with structural tailwinds:** Healthcare analytics, climate/environmental risk, infrastructure, aging population services
3. **Maintain credential optionality:** Don't commit 10 years to one exam track until the market validates it
4. **Develop AI-complementary skills:** Judgment under ambiguity, stakeholder communication, ethical reasoning, exception handling

### On the exam-heavy actuarial model:

The actuarial profession's exam system was designed in an era when computational skills were rare. Those exams test abilities that AI is increasingly capable of performing. The profession's credentialing model has not yet adapted to this reality.

## CONCLUSION

The statement that actuarial science from UAlbany starting from zero is "the most favorable position for most people" is not supported by current private sector hiring data, AI capability trajectories, or profession-specific analysis.

It may be a reasonable path for some individuals with specific characteristics and risk tolerance.

It is not a broadly optimal recommendation for someone starting from complete zero in 2025 with a 25-year planning horizon.

**More accurate statement:** "Actuarial science remains a viable career with above-average compensation for those who can pass the exams and specialize in emerging risk areas, but entry is narrowing, and the traditional path is increasingly challenged by AI capabilities."
