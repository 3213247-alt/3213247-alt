# Actuary vs. Software Engineer: A UAlbany Student's Guide

## 1. The Core Trade-Off (No Fluff)

| Feature | Actuary | Software Engineer (SWE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Barrier to Entry** | **Math/Stats Exams.** You must pass P and FM to get an internship. It is a credential-based gate. | **Skills/Portfolio.** Can you code? Can you pass a technical interview? No license required to start. |
| **The Grind** | **7-10 years of exams** while working full-time. You study 300+ hours per exam. Your 20s are largely spent studying. | **Continuous Learning.** Tech stacks change every 3-5 years. You must keep learning new frameworks/languages forever. |
| **Job Security** | **Extremely High.** Regulated industry. Insurance is mandatory. Layoffs are rare once credentialed. | **Volatile.** High hiring sprees followed by layoffs (see 2022-2024). Ageism is a real concern after 40. |
| **Salary Path** | **Predictable Steps.** Pass exam = $\$$ raise. Fellowship (FSA/FCAS) guarantees ~\$150k-\$200k+. | **Variable/High Ceiling.** Entry can be \$70k or \$150k. Seniors can make \$300k+ or stuck at \$120k depending on company. |
| **Location** | **Hub-Centric.** NYC, Hartford, Des Moines, Chicago. Less flexibility than tech. | **Anywhere (Remote friendly).** Every company needs devs. Remote work is standard in tech. |

## 2. The "UAlbany" Factor

### For Actuarial Science:
*   **Department:** Mathematics & Statistics / Business.
*   **Recruiting:** UAlbany is close to NYC and Hartford (major insurance hubs). NY Life, MetLife, Travelers often recruit from SUNY schools.
*   **Coursework:** UAlbany's classes often align directly with VEE (Validation by Educational Experience) credits. You can clear requirements just by getting a B- or better in specific classes (Econ, Finance, Stats).
*   **Networking:** The "Great Dane" alumni network is strong in NY insurance regulation and state government (Albany is the capital, home to NY DFS - Dept of Financial Services).

### For Software Engineering:
*   **Department:** College of Engineering and Applied Sciences (CEAS).
*   **Recruiting:** You compete with RPI, Stony Brook, and Buffalo. You need to stand out.
*   **The "State" Edge:** Being in Albany offers unique access to NY State ITS (Information Technology Services) jobs. Stable, pension, lower pay, but great entry point.
*   **Portfolio Pressure:** Unlike Actuarial where "Passed Exam P" is the golden ticket, as a UAlbany CS student, you **must** have side projects. Your degree alone is not enough to compete with top-tier schools.

## 3. Step-by-Step "Day in the Life" Reality

### The Actuary (Entry-Level)
1.  **Morning:** Arrive, open Excel/Prophet/R. Update monthly reserve models with new claims data.
2.  **The Work:** 80% Data cleaning and validation. "Why does this quarter's data look weird?" Investigating discrepancies.
3.  **The Tools:** Excel (Expert level), SQL, R/Python (for data manipulation), SAS.
4.  **Afternoon:** Study hours. Most companies pay you to study for 2-3 hours on work time.
5.  **Evening:** **More studying.** You have an exam in 2 months. You study 7pm-9pm.

### The Software Engineer (Entry-Level)
1.  **Morning:** Stand-up meeting (15 mins). Report progress: "I'm fixing the API endpoint for user login."
2.  **The Work:** Reading other people's code. Debugging. Writing unit tests. Maybe 20% writing "new" creative code.
3.  **The Tools:** IDE (VS Code), Git, Jira, terminal, Docker, Cloud (AWS/Azure).
4.  **Afternoon:** Code review. You submit a Pull Request, a senior dev tears it apart. You fix it.
5.  **Evening:** Free... unless you are grinding LeetCode for your next job hop or learning a new framework because your current one is dying.

## 4. The "Exam Hell" vs. "LeetCode Grind"

### Actuarial Exams (The Filter)
*   **Binary:** You pass or you fail.
*   **Volume:** ~10 exams to reach Fellowship.
*   **Cost:** Company usually pays exam fees and study materials + gives raises for passing.
*   **Risk:** If you stop passing exams, you might get fired or stuck in a "career analyst" role with a lower salary ceiling.

### Technical Interviews (The Filter)
*   **Subjective:** Depends on the interviewer's mood and the specific problem.
*   **Volume:** Hundreds of applications, dozens of online assessments (OAs).
*   **Cost:** Free, but costs your sanity.
*   **Risk:** You can be a great developer but bad at "inverting a binary tree on a whiteboard," causing you to miss top jobs.

## 5. Financial Reality Check (NY Area)

| Career Stage | Actuary (NY) | Software Engineer (NY) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internship** | \$25 - \$35 / hr | \$25 - \$50+ / hr |
| **Entry (0-2 Yrs)** | \$75k - \$95k | \$70k - \$110k (Big variance) |
| **Associate (3-5 Yrs)** | \$100k - \$130k (ASA/ACAS credential) | \$100k - \$160k |
| **Fellow/Senior (5-8 Yrs)** | \$140k - \$175k+ (FSA/FCAS) | \$140k - \$250k+ |
| **Manager/Principal (10+ Yrs)** | \$175k - \$250k+ | \$180k - \$400k+ (Stock dependent) |

*Note: SWE salaries are heavily dependent on "Total Compensation" (Stock/RSUs). Actuarial salaries are mostly base cash + bonus.*

## 6. How to Choose (Decision Matrix)

**Choose Actuary If:**
1.  **You are risk-averse.** You want a guaranteed path: Study $\to$ Pass $\to$ Raise $\to$ Job Security.
2.  **You love Statistics/Probability.** Not just "math," but specifically data distributions and financial risk.
3.  **You want work-life separation.** Once you are credentialed (Fellow), you rarely take work home.
4.  **You are okay with Excel.** It is still the king of finance.

**Choose Software Engineer If:**
1.  **You like building things.** You get a dopamine hit from "It works!"
2.  **You hate standardized tests.** The idea of studying for 10 years sounds like torture.
3.  **You want optionality.** You can work in finance, healthcare, gaming, tech, government.
4.  **You are a self-starter.** You build projects on weekends because you enjoy it, not just for a grade.

## 7. Immediate Next Steps for You (UAlbany Student)

**If leaning Actuary:**
*   **Register for Exam P or FM.** Do it now. You cannot get an internship without at least one passed.
*   **Join the Math Club.** Find study partners.

**If leaning SWE:**
*   **Build something.** A website, a game, a tool. Put it on GitHub.
*   **Data Structures & Algorithms.** Take this course seriously. It is the interview manual.
