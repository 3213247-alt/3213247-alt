## Step 2: Direct Comparison - Entry Requirements

### Actuarial Science Entry Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree required (Math, Statistics, Actuarial Science, Economics)
- UAlbany offers actuarial science program: YES
- Can major in related field: YES (Math, Statistics, Economics)

**Exams Required:**
- Exam P (Probability): Must pass before or during college
- Exam FM (Financial Mathematics): Must pass before or during college
- Total exams to Associate (ASA/ACAS): 5-7 exams
- Total exams to Fellowship (FSA/FCAS): 7-10 exams
- Timeline: 7-10 years of exams while working

**Skills Required:**
- Strong mathematics (calculus, probability, statistics)
- Programming increasingly required (Python, R, SQL)
- Excel/VBA proficiency
- Business communication skills

**First Job Requirements (2025):**
- 2-3 exams passed minimum
- Internship experience (85% of hires have internship)
- Programming skills (Python/R)
- GPA: 3.4+ typically required
- Geographic flexibility (Hartford, Des Moines, Chicago, NYC)

### Software Engineering Entry Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree common but not always required
- UAlbany offers computer science program: YES
- Can major in related field: YES (Math, Physics, Engineering)
- Bootcamp alternative: YES (6-12 months, $10K-$20K)

**Certifications:**
- No mandatory certifications
- Optional: AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure certifications
- Optional: Coding bootcamp certificates

**Skills Required:**
- Programming languages (varies by role: Python, Java, JavaScript, C++, etc.)
- Data structures and algorithms
- Software development practices (Git, testing, debugging)
- Problem-solving ability

**First Job Requirements (2025):**
- Portfolio of projects (GitHub)
- Technical interview performance
- Internship helpful but not mandatory
- Degree OR bootcamp OR self-taught with strong portfolio
- Geographic flexibility: HIGH (jobs in most cities)

## Step 5: Direct Comparison - Career Timeline

### Actuarial Science Timeline

**Years 1-2 (College):**
- Pass Exam P and Exam FM
- Complete VEE requirements
- Secure internship (critical)
- Learn Python/R basics
- Apply to 40-60 internships

**Years 3-4 (First Job):**
- Entry-level actuarial analyst role
- Pass 1-2 more exams per year
- Learn actuarial software (Prophet, AXIS)
- Build modeling skills
- Salary: $65K-$85K

**Years 5-7 (Associate Level):**
- Achieve ASA/ACAS credential
- Specialize in practice area
- Take on more complex projects
- Salary: $95K-$130K

**Years 8-12 (Fellowship Level):**
- Achieve FSA/FCAS credential
- Senior actuarial roles
- Strategic decision-making
- Salary: $140K-$200K

**Years 13+ (Senior/Management):**
- Management or senior technical roles
- Board-level influence possible
- Salary: $180K-$300K+

**Total Time to Peak: 12-15 years**

### Software Engineering Timeline

**Years 1-2 (College/Bootcamp):**
- Complete degree or bootcamp
- Build portfolio (5-10 projects)
- Learn core technologies
- Apply to internships or entry-level roles
- Practice technical interviews

**Years 3-4 (First Job):**
- Junior software engineer role
- Learn company tech stack
- Build production experience
- Salary: $70K-$120K

**Years 5-7 (Mid-Level):**
- Mid-level engineer
- Take on larger projects
- Mentor junior engineers
- Consider job change for salary boost
- Salary: $100K-$180K

**Years 8-12 (Senior Level):**
- Senior engineer or tech lead
- Architecture decisions
- Cross-team collaboration
- Salary: $140K-$250K

**Years 13+ (Staff/Principal):**
- Staff/Principal engineer or engineering manager
- Strategic technical decisions
- Salary: $200K-$400K+

**Total Time to Peak: 8-12 years**

## Step 6: Direct Comparison - Work-Life Balance

### Actuarial Science Work-Life Balance

**During Exam Years (Years 1-10):**
- Work: 40-45 hours/week
- Study: 15-20 hours/week
- Total: 55-65 hours/week
- Weekends: Often studying
- Vacation: Planned around exam dates
- Social life: Limited during exam periods

**After Exams (Years 10+):**
- Work: 40-50 hours/week typical
- Study: Minimal (continuing education)
- Total: 40-50 hours/week
- Weekends: Free
- Vacation: Normal scheduling
- Social life: Normal

**Stress Factors:**
- Exam pressure (7-10 years)
- Failing exams = career impact
- Work deadlines + exam studying
- Balancing both is difficult

**Positive Factors:**
- Predictable hours (not consulting/banking)
- Less age discrimination
- Job security during recessions
- Remote work increasing

### Software Engineering Work-Life Balance

**Early Career (Years 1-5):**
- Work: 40-50 hours/week typical
- Learning: 5-10 hours/week (self-study)
- Total: 45-60 hours/week
- Weekends: Usually free (unless deadline)
- On-call: Possible (varies by company)
- Social life: Normal

**Mid-Career (Years 5-10):**
- Work: 40-50 hours/week typical
- Learning: 3-5 hours/week (staying current)
- Total: 43-55 hours/week
- On-call: Less common (senior roles)
- Social life: Normal

**Senior Career (Years 10+):**
- Work: 40-50 hours/week typical
- Learning: 2-5 hours/week
- Total: 42-55 hours/week
- More meetings, less coding
- Social life: Normal

**Stress Factors:**
- Tight deadlines
- On-call rotations (some companies)
- Rapid technology changes
- Age discrimination (some companies)
- Layoffs (tech companies)

**Positive Factors:**
- No mandatory exams
- Flexible hours (many companies)
- Remote work very common
- High demand = job security (if skilled)
