# Actuary vs. Software Engineering: Step-by-Step Decision Framework for UAlbany Student

## Step 1: Understand What You're Actually Choosing

### Actuary Career Path
- **What it is**: Risk assessment and financial modeling for insurance companies
- **Entry requirement**: Pass actuarial exams (SOA or CAS) - 5-10 exams over 7-10 years
- **Typical work**: Pricing insurance products, reserving, regulatory compliance, statistical modeling
- **Career progression**: Exam-gated (must pass exams to advance)
- **Geographic concentration**: Hartford CT, Des Moines IA, Chicago IL, NYC, Boston MA

### Software Engineering Career Path
- **What it is**: Building software applications, systems, and platforms
- **Entry requirement**: Bachelor's degree + programming skills (no mandatory exams)
- **Typical work**: Writing code, debugging, system design, collaborating with teams
- **Career progression**: Skill and performance-based (no mandatory credentials)
- **Geographic options**: Every major city, strong remote work options

## Step 3: Compare Key Factors Side-by-Side

### Factor 1: Entry Difficulty

**Actuary:**
- Must pass actuarial exams (Exam P, FM, etc.) before or during job search
- Entry-level positions: 30-40% reduction since 2015
- Competition: High (10-15% internship acceptance rates)
- Typical path: 2-3 exams + internship → first job
- **Difficulty: HIGH** (4/10 attractiveness for entry-level)

**Software Engineering:**
- No mandatory exams or credentials
- Entry-level positions: Abundant (though competitive)
- Competition: Moderate-High (varies by location/company)
- Typical path: Degree + portfolio projects → first job
- **Difficulty: MODERATE** (6.5/10 attractiveness for entry-level)

**Winner: Software Engineering** (easier to break into)

---

### Factor 2: Early Career Compensation (Years 1-5)

**Actuary:**
- Year 1: $60K-$75K (with 2-3 exams)
- Year 3: $75K-$95K (with 4-5 exams)
- Year 5: $95K-$120K (with 6-7 exams, approaching Associate level)
- **Note**: Raises tied to exam passing (not just performance)

**Software Engineering:**
- Year 1: $70K-$100K (varies by location/company)
- Year 3: $90K-$130K (with experience)
- Year 5: $110K-$160K (or higher at tech companies)
- **Note**: Raises tied to performance and skill development

**Winner: Software Engineering** (higher early career pay, faster growth)

## Step 8: Hybrid Paths to Consider

- **Pros**: Higher early career pay, test if you like quantitative work

- **Verdict**: Not recommended (exams get harder as you age, better to start early)

### Option 2: Start Actuary, Pivot to Software Engineering Later
- **Pros**: Strong mathematical foundation, risk management skills transfer
- **Cons**: Wasted exam time/money, lower early career pay
- **Verdict**: Possible but inefficient (better to choose upfront)

### Option 3: Quantitative Developer / Actuarial Software Developer
- **What it is**: Software engineering role at insurance companies building actuarial tools
- **Pros**: Combines both skills, good pay, stable industry
- **Cons**: Niche role, fewer positions available
- **Verdict**: Good option if you can't decide (requires both skills)

### Option 4: Data Science / Analytics (Middle Ground)
- **What it is**: Statistical analysis and modeling (like actuary) but no exams required
- **Pros**: Uses math/stats skills, no mandatory exams, good pay
- **Cons**: Less stable than actuary, more competitive than software engineering
- **Verdict**: Consider if you like the math but hate exams

## Step 12: When to Reconsider Your Choice

### Exit Signals for Actuary Path:

**Year 1-2:**
- Failed Exam P or FM 3 times → STOP, reconsider
- Can't secure internship after 2 summers → Consider alternatives
- Hate the exam studying process → Wrong career

**Year 3-5:**
- Can't get first job 18+ months searching → Pivot to data science/risk management
- Failing multiple exams (3+ fails) → Assess realistically
- Salary <$70K year 3 → Company/role problem

### Exit Signals for Software Engineering Path:

**Year 1-2:**
- Can't get first job 12+ months searching → Improve skills, consider bootcamp
- Consistently frustrated by coding → May not be right fit
- No improvement in coding ability after 2 years → Reconsider

**Year 3-5:**
- Salary stagnant <$80K year 5 → May need to switch companies/roles
- Career satisfaction low → Consider specialization or pivot
- Age discrimination concerns → Plan for long-term (consider management track)
