## Part 1: Career Path Definitions

### Regulatory Medical Writer

**What It Is:**
- Creates scientific documents for drug/device regulatory submissions
- Documents include: Clinical Study Reports, regulatory submission modules, investigator brochures, compliance documents
- Submits to: FDA (U.S.), EMA (Europe), Health Canada, PMDA (Japan), other global agencies
- Industry: Pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies

**Entry Requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree (life sciences preferred: biology, chemistry, pharmacology, or related)
- Some positions require master's degree or PhD
- Writing samples and technical writing ability
- Understanding of regulatory guidelines (ICH, FDA, EMA)
- Certification available but not required (RAPS, AMWA)

**Career Progression:**
- Entry-level: Medical Writer (0-2 years)
- Mid-level: Senior Medical Writer (2-5 years)
- Senior: Principal Medical Writer (5-10 years)
- Lead: Medical Writing Manager/Director (10+ years)
- Alternative: Freelance/consultant (after 5+ years experience)

### Actuary

**What It Is:**
- Analyzes financial risk using mathematics, statistics, and financial theory
- Works in: Insurance (life, health, property & casualty), pensions, consulting, government
- Primary function: Calculate insurance premiums, reserves, and financial risk
- Tied to: U.S. insurance markets, U.S. stock market performance, U.S. economic conditions

**Entry Requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree (actuarial science, mathematics, statistics, or related)
- Actuarial exams: SOA (Society of Actuaries) or CAS (Casualty Actuarial Society)
- Entry-level: 1-2 exams passed
- Associate level (ASA/ACAS): 5-7 exams (3-5 years)
- Fellowship level (FSA/FCAS): 8-10 exams (5-10 years total)

**Career Progression:**
- Entry-level: Actuarial Analyst (0-2 years, 1-2 exams)
- Mid-level: Actuarial Associate (2-5 years, 3-5 exams)
- Senior: Senior Actuary (5-10 years, Associate credential)
- Lead: Actuarial Manager/Director (10+ years, Fellowship credential)
- Alternative: Consulting actuary (after Fellowship)

## Part 2: Geographic Mobility Analysis

### Regulatory Medical Writer: High Mobility

**Mobility Factors:**

1. **Global Industry Presence**
   - Pharmaceutical/biotech companies operate in: U.S., U.K., Switzerland, Germany, France, Japan, Canada, Australia, Singapore, India, China
   - Regulatory agencies exist in all major markets: FDA (U.S.), EMA (Europe), MHRA (U.K.), Health Canada, PMDA (Japan), TGA (Australia)

2. **Transferable Skills**
   - Regulatory guidelines are standardized globally (ICH guidelines used worldwide)
   - English is primary language for regulatory documents
   - Writing skills transfer across countries
   - Regulatory knowledge (FDA, EMA) applicable in multiple markets

3. **Work Authorization**
   - Many countries have skilled worker visa programs for medical writers
   - U.K.: Skilled Worker Visa (Tier 2)
   - Canada: Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker)
   - Australia: Skilled Independent Visa
   - Switzerland: Work permits for qualified professionals
   - EU: Blue Card (for non-EU citizens)

4. **Remote Work Potential**
   - High: Regulatory writing can be done remotely
   - Many companies offer remote positions
   - Freelance opportunities allow location independence

**Mobility Limitation:**
- Must understand local regulatory requirements (FDA vs. EMA differences)
- Some positions require in-country presence for regulatory meetings
- Language barriers in non-English speaking countries (though English often sufficient)

### Actuary: Low Mobility (Tied to U.S.)

**Mobility Factors:**

1. **Country-Specific Credentials**
   - U.S. actuaries hold SOA or CAS credentials
   - U.K. actuaries: Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA)
   - Canada: Canadian Institute of Actuaries (CIA)
   - Australia: Actuaries Institute Australia
   - Each country requires different exam series and credentials
   - Credentials do not transfer directly between countries

2. **U.S. Market Dependency**
   - Actuaries primarily work with U.S. insurance companies
   - U.S. insurance regulations (state-by-state) are unique
   - U.S. stock market performance directly impacts actuarial work (pension funds, investments)
   - U.S. healthcare system structure is unique (Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance)

3. **Limited International Transfer**
   - Moving to another country requires:
     - Passing that country's actuarial exams (starting over)
     - Learning new regulatory systems
     - Rebuilding professional network
     - 2-5 years to re-establish credentials

4. **Remote Work Limitation**
   - Lower: Many actuarial roles require in-office presence
   - Regulatory compliance requires U.S. presence
   - Client meetings and regulatory interactions are location-dependent

**Mobility Limitation:**
- Actuarial credentials are country-specific
- U.S. actuarial work is tied to U.S. economy and stock market
- Cannot easily switch countries without restarting credential process
- Economic downturn in U.S. affects all U.S. actuaries regardless of location

## Part 4: Job Security During Economic Downturns

### Regulatory Medical Writer: Moderate-High Risk During Depressions

**Risk Factors:**

1. **Pharmaceutical Industry Dependency**
   - Pharmaceutical companies cut R&D spending during economic downturns
   - Drug development projects delayed or cancelled
   - Regulatory submissions decrease
   - Medical writing positions eliminated

2. **Historical Evidence:**
   - 2008-2009: Pharmaceutical companies reduced hiring, some layoffs
   - 2020 COVID-19: Initial uncertainty, then recovery (due to vaccine development)
   - Industry is cyclical but less volatile than many sectors

3. **Protection Factors:**
   - Essential drugs still need regulatory approval
   - Generic drug submissions continue
   - Regulatory compliance cannot be eliminated
   - Some companies maintain regulatory teams even during cuts

4. **Unemployment Risk:**
   - Moderate: 10-20% unemployment risk during major depression
   - Lower during moderate recessions: 5-10% risk
   - Recovery: 6-12 months after economic recovery begins

**Mobility Advantage During Downturns:**
- Can seek opportunities in countries with stronger economies
- Global pharmaceutical industry (not all countries decline simultaneously)
- Remote work allows serving clients in multiple countries
- Freelance opportunities provide flexibility

### Actuary: Low Risk During Depressions

**Risk Factors:**

1. **Insurance Industry Stability**
   - Insurance is essential service (people need insurance regardless of economy)
   - Insurance companies are heavily regulated and required to maintain reserves
   - Actuarial work is legally required (reserve calculations, pricing)
   - Cannot eliminate actuarial functions due to regulatory requirements

2. **Historical Evidence:**
   - 2008-2009: Actuaries had lower unemployment than general population
   - Actuarial profession maintained stability during Great Depression
   - Insurance industry is counter-cyclical in some ways (more claims, more need for actuaries)

3. **Protection Factors:**
   - Regulatory requirement: Insurance companies must employ actuaries
   - Reserve calculations are legally mandated
   - Actuarial credentials create barriers to entry (job protection)
   - Actuarial work is essential for insurance company operations

4. **Unemployment Risk:**
   - Low: 2-5% unemployment risk during major depression
   - Very low during moderate recessions: 1-3% risk
   - Recovery: Immediate (actuaries needed throughout downturn)

**Mobility Limitation During Downturns:**
- Cannot easily move to another country (credentials don't transfer)
- Tied to U.S. economy (if U.S. declines, all U.S. actuaries affected)
- Limited ability to seek opportunities elsewhere
- Must wait for U.S. economic recovery

## Part 6: Career Path Requirements

### Regulatory Medical Writer: Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree: 3 years (UAlbany, free)
- Preferred: Master's degree or PhD (adds 2-4 years, $30,000-$100,000 cost)
- Alternative: Bachelor's + experience (possible but slower progression)

**Certifications (Optional but Helpful):**
- RAPS (Regulatory Affairs Professional Society): $500-$1,500
- AMWA (American Medical Writers Association): $200-$500
- Study time: 3-6 months per certification

**Skills Development:**
- Technical writing: 1-2 years to develop
- Regulatory knowledge: 6-12 months learning ICH, FDA, EMA guidelines
- Scientific knowledge: Ongoing (depends on therapeutic areas)

**Time to Competency:**
- Entry-level ready: 3 years (bachelor's completion)
- Mid-level (5 years): 8 years total (3 bachelor's + 5 experience)
- Senior level (10 years): 13 years total

**Total Investment:**
- Education: $0 (free bachelor's) or $30,000-$100,000 (with master's)
- Certifications: $0-$2,000 (optional)
- Time: 3-13 years to senior level

### Actuary: Requirements

**Education:**
- Bachelor's degree: 3 years (UAlbany, free)
- UAlbany advantage: Direct actuarial program (only 40 universities in U.S.)

**Actuarial Exams (Required):**
- SOA (Society of Actuaries) track:
  - Exam P (Probability): 3-6 months study
  - Exam FM (Financial Mathematics): 3-6 months study
  - Exam IFM (Investments and Financial Markets): 3-6 months study
  - Exam SRM (Statistics for Risk Modeling): 3-6 months study
  - Exam STAM (Short-Term Actuarial Mathematics): 3-6 months study
  - Exam LTAM (Long-Term Actuarial Mathematics): 3-6 months study
  - FAP (Fundamentals of Actuarial Practice): 6-12 months
  - APC (Associateship Professionalism Course): 1-2 days
  - ASA credential: 5-7 exams total (3-5 years)
  - Fellowship: Additional 3 exams (2-3 more years)

- CAS (Casualty Actuarial Society) track:
  - Similar exam structure for property & casualty insurance
  - 6-10 exams total for Fellowship

**Exam Costs:**
- Each exam: $200-$500 (registration fee)
- Study materials: $200-$800 per exam
- Total exam costs: $3,000-$8,000 (for Associate level)
- Additional: $2,000-$5,000 (for Fellowship)

**Skills Development:**
- Mathematical/statistical skills: Developed through exams
- Programming: Excel, VBA, R, Python (learned on job)
- Insurance knowledge: Learned on job

**Time to Competency:**
- Entry-level ready: 3 years (bachelor's + 1-2 exams)
- Associate level (ASA/ACAS): 6-8 years total (3 bachelor's + 3-5 exam years)
- Fellowship level (FSA/FCAS): 8-11 years total

**Total Investment:**
- Education: $0 (free bachelor's)
- Exams: $3,000-$13,000 (Associate to Fellowship)
- Study time: 3-11 years to Fellowship
- Opportunity cost: Lower starting salary while studying (but exams increase salary)
