# Step-by-Step Decision Framework: Actuary vs Software Engineering
## For UAlbany Student

## Step 1: Understand What Each Career Actually Does (Day-to-Day Reality)

### Actuary Daily Work:
- **Entry-level (Years 1-3)**: 
  - Data cleaning and preparation (60% of time)
  - Running actuarial models in Excel/Python
  - Creating reports and presentations
  - Reconciling numbers and fixing errors
  - Studying for actuarial exams (15-20 hours/week outside work)
  
- **Mid-level (Years 4-10)**:
  - Building and validating actuarial models
  - Setting assumptions for pricing/reserving
  - Regulatory reporting (statutory filings)
  - Communicating results to business stakeholders
  - Managing junior actuaries
  - Still studying for exams (until Fellowship achieved)

- **Senior (Years 10+)**:
  - Strategic risk decisions
  - Board-level presentations
  - Regulatory compliance oversight
  - Business strategy input
  - Less technical work, more communication/strategy

### Software Engineer Daily Work:
- **Entry-level (Years 1-3)**:
  - Writing code to build features
  - Fixing bugs
  - Code reviews
  - Testing code
  - Learning new technologies
  - Attending standup meetings
  - Deploying code to production

- **Mid-level (Years 4-10)**:
  - Designing system architecture
  - Leading feature development
  - Mentoring junior engineers
  - Technical decision-making
  - Performance optimization
  - Cross-team collaboration

- **Senior (Years 10+)**:
  - System design and architecture
  - Technical leadership
  - Strategic technical decisions
  - May move to management or stay technical
  - Less coding, more design/review

**Key Difference**: Actuaries spend significant time studying for exams (7-10 years). Software engineers spend time learning new technologies but it's integrated into work, not separate exam studying.

## Step 2: Assess Your Personal Fit (Answer These Honestly)

### Mathematical Ability Assessment:

**For Actuary:**
- Can you pass calculus courses (Calc I, II, III) with A or B?
- Do you enjoy probability and statistics?
- Can you solve complex mathematical problems independently?
- Are you comfortable with abstract mathematical concepts?

**For Software Engineering:**
- Can you think logically and systematically?
- Do you enjoy breaking down problems into steps?
- Can you learn programming languages?
- Are you comfortable with debugging and troubleshooting?

**Action**: Take a practice actuarial exam (Exam P) and a basic programming course (Python 101). See which feels more natural.

### Persistence and Discipline:

**For Actuary:**
- Can you study 15-20 hours/week for 7-10 years while working full-time?
- Can you handle failing exams and retaking them?
- Are you okay with delayed gratification (exams before raises)?

**For Software Engineering:**
- Can you continuously learn new technologies?
- Are you okay with technologies becoming obsolete?
- Can you handle debugging frustrating problems?

**Action**: Try studying for Exam P for 3 months (200-300 hours). If you can't maintain this, actuarial path will be very difficult.

### Work Style Preferences:

**Actuary:**
- More structured, regulated environment
- Clear career progression tied to exams
- More predictable work (insurance industry stable)
- Less creative freedom
- More analytical, less building

**Software Engineering:**
- More flexible, fast-changing environment
- Career progression based on skills/projects
- Less predictable (tech layoffs, industry changes)
- More creative problem-solving
- More building/creating

**Action**: Do you prefer structure and predictability, or flexibility and rapid change?

## Step 3: Compare Financial Outcomes (Real Numbers)

### Actuary Compensation (2025 Dollars):

**Entry-Level (0-2 years, 2-3 exams):**
- Starting salary: $65,000 - $85,000
- With internship: $70,000 - $90,000
- Without internship: $60,000 - $75,000 (harder to get job)

**Mid-Level (3-7 years, ASA/ACAS achieved):**
- Salary: $100,000 - $140,000
- Exam raises: $5,000 - $10,000 per exam passed
- Total compensation: $110,000 - $160,000

**Senior (8+ years, FSA/FCAS):**
- Salary: $150,000 - $250,000
- VP/Director level: $200,000 - $350,000
- Total compensation: $180,000 - $400,000

**Lifetime Earnings Estimate (40-year career):**
- If successful (FSA/FCAS): $12-15 million
- If partial success (ASA/ACAS only): $8-10 million
- If fail to enter profession: $5-6 million (similar to general business)

### Software Engineer Compensation (2025 Dollars):

**Entry-Level (0-2 years):**
- Starting salary: $70,000 - $120,000
- FAANG companies: $120,000 - $180,000 (total comp)
- Startups: $60,000 - $100,000 (equity potential)
- Other companies: $70,000 - $100,000

**Mid-Level (3-7 years):**
- Salary: $120,000 - $180,000
- FAANG: $200,000 - $350,000 (total comp)
- Other companies: $120,000 - $160,000

**Senior (8+ years):**
- Salary: $160,000 - $250,000
- FAANG: $300,000 - $600,000 (total comp)
- Staff/Principal level: $250,000 - $500,000+
- Management track: $200,000 - $400,000+

**Lifetime Earnings Estimate (40-year career):**
- FAANG path: $15-25 million (if you stay)
- Other tech companies: $10-15 million
- Non-tech companies: $8-12 million
- Note: Tech has higher variance (layoffs, ageism risk)

**Financial Comparison:**
- **Early career (Years 1-5)**: Software engineering pays more
- **Mid-career (Years 6-15)**: Similar, depends on path
- **Late career (Years 16-40)**: Actuary more stable, software engineering higher ceiling but more risk
- **Lifetime**: Similar if both successful, but actuarial more predictable

## Step 4: Assess Job Market Reality (2025-2030)

### Actuary Job Market:

**Entry-Level Reality:**
- **Number of positions**: Limited (insurance industry concentrated)
- **Competition**: High (10-15 applicants per entry-level position)
- **Requirements**: 2-3 exams + internship typically needed
- **Geographic constraints**: Must be near insurance hubs (Hartford CT, Des Moines IA, Chicago IL, NYC, Boston MA)
- **Trend**: Entry-level positions decreasing 30-40% since 2015 due to automation

**Mid-Level Market:**
- **Number of positions**: Moderate
- **Competition**: Medium (fewer qualified candidates)
- **Requirements**: ASA/ACAS credential
- **Geographic flexibility**: More options, some remote work

**Senior Market:**
- **Number of positions**: Limited but stable
- **Competition**: Low (few qualified candidates)
- **Requirements**: FSA/FCAS credential
- **Geographic flexibility**: Good, remote work common

**Job Security:**
- **Recession resistance**: High (insurance industry essential)
- **Automation risk**: Medium (entry-level tasks automated, but strategic roles remain)
- **Age discrimination**: Low (actuaries work into 60s+)
- **Industry stability**: High (insurance always needed)

### Software Engineering Job Market:

**Entry-Level Reality:**
- **Number of positions**: High (many companies need developers)
- **Competition**: Medium-High (many bootcamp grads, CS majors compete)
- **Requirements**: Portfolio, coding skills, sometimes degree
- **Geographic constraints**: Low (remote work common, jobs everywhere)
- **Trend**: Growing, but entry-level becoming more competitive

**Mid-Level Market:**
- **Number of positions**: Very high
- **Competition**: Medium (many qualified candidates)
- **Requirements**: 3-5 years experience, proven skills
- **Geographic flexibility**: Excellent (remote work standard)

**Senior Market:**
- **Number of positions**: High
- **Competition**: Low-Medium (fewer qualified candidates)
- **Requirements**: 7+ years experience, system design skills
- **Geographic flexibility**: Excellent

**Job Security:**
- **Recession resistance**: Low-Medium (tech layoffs common in downturns)
- **Automation risk**: Low-Medium (AI helps but doesn't replace yet)
- **Age discrimination**: Medium-High (tech favors younger workers)
- **Industry stability**: Medium (tech changes rapidly, companies rise/fall)

**Job Market Comparison:**
- **Easier to get first job**: Software engineering (more positions)
- **Easier to change jobs**: Software engineering (more opportunities)
- **More stable long-term**: Actuary (less layoff risk)
- **Better for 40-year career**: Actuary (less age discrimination)

## Step 5: Evaluate Time Investment and Career Timeline

### Actuary Timeline:

**Undergraduate (4 years):**
- Year 1-2: Pass Exam P (Probability)
- Year 3: Pass Exam FM (Financial Mathematics), secure internship
- Year 4: Pass one more exam, apply for full-time positions

**Early Career (Years 1-5 post-graduation):**
- Pass 2-3 more exams
- Achieve ASA/ACAS credential (typically Year 4-5)
- Study 15-20 hours/week outside work
- Limited social life during exam periods

**Mid-Career (Years 6-10):**
- Continue to FSA/FCAS credential
- Pass remaining 3-4 exams
- Still studying 15-20 hours/week
- Career progression tied to exam completion

**Total Time Investment:**
- **Exams**: 1,000-1,500 hours of studying over 7-10 years
- **Equivalent to**: Part-time job for 7-10 years
- **Career establishment**: 5-7 years to reach stable mid-career

### Software Engineering Timeline:

**Undergraduate (4 years):**
- Year 1-2: Learn programming fundamentals, data structures
- Year 3: Internship, build portfolio projects
- Year 4: Apply for full-time positions, continue building skills

**Early Career (Years 1-5 post-graduation):**
- Learn on the job
- Build skills through projects
- No separate exam studying
- Can change jobs easily for growth

**Mid-Career (Years 6-10):**
- Specialize in area (backend, frontend, DevOps, etc.)
- Learn new technologies as needed
- Career progression based on skills/projects
- Continuous learning integrated into work

**Total Time Investment:**
- **Learning**: Continuous but integrated into work
- **No separate exam studying**: Learning happens during work hours
- **Career establishment**: 3-5 years to reach stable mid-career

**Timeline Comparison:**
- **Faster to establish**: Software engineering (no exam barrier)
- **More time-consuming early career**: Actuary (exams + work)
- **Better work-life balance early**: Software engineering
- **Better work-life balance later**: Actuary (after exams done)

## Step 7: Assess Risk and Uncertainty

### Actuary Risks:

**High Probability Risks:**
1. **Can't get first job** (35% of exam-passers struggle)
   - Mitigation: Multiple internships, networking, geographic flexibility
   - Impact: Delayed career start, may need to pivot

2. **Fail exams repeatedly** (40% don't reach FSA/FCAS)
   - Mitigation: Study 300+ hours per exam, coaching
   - Impact: Career stagnation, wasted time

3. **Automation reduces entry-level jobs** (trending)
   - Mitigation: Learn programming, focus on strategic roles
   - Impact: Harder to enter profession

**Medium Probability Risks:**
4. **Don't enjoy the work** (discover after 2-3 years)
   - Impact: Sunk cost, difficult to pivot

5. **Insurance industry disruption** (InsurTech, AI)
   - Impact: Job market changes, need to adapt

### Software Engineering Risks:

**High Probability Risks:**
1. **Tech layoffs** (common in downturns)
   - Mitigation: Keep skills current, build emergency fund
   - Impact: Job loss, need to find new position

2. **Age discrimination** (tech favors younger workers)
   - Mitigation: Stay current with technologies, consider management
   - Impact: Harder to find jobs in 40s-50s

3. **Technology obsolescence** (skills become outdated)
   - Mitigation: Continuous learning
   - Impact: Need to constantly reskill

**Medium Probability Risks:**
4. **Burnout** (fast-paced, high-pressure environments)
   - Impact: Career change, health issues

5. **Industry disruption** (AI, automation)
   - Impact: Job market changes, need to adapt

**Risk Comparison:**
- **More stable long-term**: Actuary (less layoff risk, less age discrimination)
- **More risk early career**: Actuary (harder to get first job)
- **More risk mid-career**: Software engineering (layoffs, ageism)
- **More predictable**: Actuary (clear path, exam-gated)

## Final Step: Make Your Decision

**After completing all steps above:**

1. **Take action on both paths** (don't just think, do)
2. **Set a deadline** (end of sophomore year)
