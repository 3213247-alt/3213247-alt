## PART 1: CAREER DEFINITIONS

### Actuary
- Calculates financial risk for insurance companies, pension funds, and financial institutions
- Uses mathematics, statistics, and financial theory to assess probability of events
- Works primarily for insurance companies, consulting firms, government agencies

### Regulatory Medical Writer
- Creates scientific documents for drug and medical device approvals
- Writes Clinical Study Reports, FDA/EMA submissions, Investigator Brochures
- Works for pharmaceutical companies, biotech firms, Contract Research Organizations (CROs)

## PART 4: GEOGRAPHIC MOBILITY ANALYSIS

### Actuary: LOW MOBILITY

**Tied to U.S. Economy Because:**
1. Actuarial credentials are country-specific
2. SOA/CAS certifications recognized primarily in U.S. and Canada
3. Insurance regulations differ by country
4. Exam systems differ globally (UK uses IFoA, Europe uses various national bodies)
5. Switching countries requires re-certification (2-5 years additional exams)
6. Pension/Social Security calculations are U.S.-specific
7. Most actuarial employers are domestic insurance companies

**If U.S. Economy Declines:**
- Limited options to relocate
- Must re-certify in new country
- Skills not directly transferable
- Language barrier compounds credential barrier

### Regulatory Medical Writer: HIGH MOBILITY

**Can Work Globally Because:**
1. Drug approval processes follow similar structures worldwide (ICH Guidelines)
2. Clinical Study Reports use standardized format globally
3. English is the international language of pharmaceutical submissions
4. Skills transfer directly between FDA (U.S.), EMA (Europe), PMDA (Japan), TGA (Australia)
5. No country-specific certification required
6. Pharmaceutical companies are multinational
7. Remote work is standard in this field

**If U.S. Economy Declines:**
- Can relocate to Europe (Switzerland, UK, Germany, Ireland)
- Can relocate to Asia (Singapore, Japan)
- Can work remotely for companies in stable economies
- Same skills, same documents, same regulatory framework

## PART 5: JOB SECURITY DURING ECONOMIC DOWNTURNS

### Actuary: MORE STABLE DURING RECESSIONS

**Why Actuaries Keep Jobs During Downturns:**
1. Insurance is mandatory (auto, health, home)
2. Risk assessment becomes MORE important during uncertainty
3. Pension funds still need management
4. Regulatory requirements don't pause
5. Companies cannot operate without actuaries (legal requirement)

**Historical Data:**
- 2008 Financial Crisis: Actuary unemployment stayed under 3%
- 2020 COVID-19: Actuary unemployment stayed under 4%
- Actuaries rank consistently in "most recession-proof jobs" lists

### Regulatory Medical Writer: MODERATE STABILITY

**Why Medical Writers May Lose Jobs During Downturns:**
1. Pharmaceutical companies cut R&D budgets during recessions
2. Clinical trials get delayed or cancelled
3. Companies freeze hiring for non-essential roles
4. Contract/freelance writers cut first

**But Also Protected Because:**
1. Drug approvals are time-sensitive (patent clocks)
2. Regulatory deadlines don't change during recessions
3. Healthcare spending remains relatively stable
4. Aging population drives continuous drug development

**Historical Data:**
- 2008 Financial Crisis: Pharma industry laid off 10-15% of workforce
- 2020 COVID-19: Medical writers in HIGH demand (vaccine development)
- Typical recession: 15-25% reduction in pharma hiring

6. **Remote Work Trend:** Medical writing has embraced remote work permanently. This creates location arbitrage opportunities.

7. **Freelance Option:** Senior medical writers can earn $100-150/hour freelancing. Actuaries rarely have freelance options.

8. **Industry Concentration:** Actuary jobs concentrated in Hartford, NYC, Chicago, Des Moines. Medical writer jobs spread across more cities and fully remote.

9. **Language Advantage:** If you speak other languages, medical writing gains more value (EU submissions). Actuarial work rarely benefits from multilingual skills.

10. **Career Pivot Options:** Medical writer can pivot to medical communications, marketing, or healthcare consulting. Actuary pivots are limited to data science or finance.
