## SECTION 2: ACTUARY CAREER PATH

### Entry Requirements
- Bachelor's degree in Actuarial Science (UAlbany offers this)
- Pass SOA/CAS exams (7-10 exams over 5-10 years while working)
- U.S. credential: FSA (Fellow of Society of Actuaries) or FCAS

### Salary Trajectory (2029 U.S. Dollars, inflation-adjusted estimates)
| Career Stage | Years | Annual Salary |
|--------------|-------|---------------|
| Entry Level (0-3 exams) | 0-3 | $65,000-$85,000 |
| Mid-Level (4-6 exams) | 3-7 | $90,000-$130,000 |
| Credentialed (FSA/FCAS) | 7-12 | $130,000-$180,000 |
| Senior/Management | 12-25 | $180,000-$300,000 |
| Executive/Chief Actuary | 25-40 | $300,000-$500,000+ |

### Job Security
- **U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics:** 21% growth projected 2021-2031 (much faster than average)
- **Unemployment rate for actuaries:** Historically below 2%
- **During 2008-2009 recession:** Actuary unemployment remained under 3%
- **During COVID-2020:** Minimal layoffs in actuarial field

### Geographic Mobility Problem (CRITICAL)
- Actuarial credentials are **country-specific**
- U.S. SOA/CAS exams ≠ UK Institute and Faculty of Actuaries
- U.S. SOA/CAS exams ≠ European actuarial bodies
- **To work in another country:** Must pass that country's exams again (2-5 years additional)
- **Mutual Recognition Agreements:** Exist but limited; still require additional exams
- **Languages:** Insurance regulations are jurisdiction-specific; must learn local laws

### If U.S. Economy Collapses
- Insurance companies are domestic-focused
- Your skills are tied to U.S. insurance regulations
- Moving abroad means: new exams, new regulatory knowledge, 2-5 year restart
- **Realistic mobility time:** 3-5 years to become employable in new country

## SECTION 3: REGULATORY MEDICAL WRITER CAREER PATH

### Entry Requirements
- Bachelor's degree (science preferred, but not required)
- Many enter with life sciences, pharmacy, nursing, or English degrees
- No mandatory certification (AMWA certification optional but helpful)
- On-the-job training common

### Salary Trajectory (2029 U.S. Dollars, inflation-adjusted estimates)
| Career Stage | Years | Annual Salary |
|--------------|-------|---------------|
| Entry Level | 0-3 | $55,000-$75,000 |
| Mid-Level | 3-7 | $75,000-$100,000 |
| Senior Writer | 7-12 | $100,000-$140,000 |
| Principal/Lead | 12-20 | $140,000-$180,000 |
| Director/Freelance Premium | 20-40 | $180,000-$250,000 |

### Job Security
- Pharmaceutical industry is global and growing
- Aging populations worldwide = more drug development
- Regulatory requirements are increasing, not decreasing
- **Recession vulnerability:** Moderate. Drug development slows in severe recessions.
- **2008-2009:** Some layoffs, but less than other industries
- **COVID-2020:** Industry boomed (vaccine development)

### Geographic Mobility Advantage (CRITICAL)
- ICH (International Council for Harmonisation) standardized documents globally
- Clinical Study Report format is **identical** in U.S., EU, Japan, Canada, Australia
- FDA, EMA, PMDA, Health Canada use same document structures
- **English is the global language** of pharmaceutical regulatory submissions
- **To work in another country:** Update resume, apply, start work within weeks
- Moving from U.S. to EU: No retraining required
- Moving from U.S. to Asia (Singapore, Japan): English documents still needed

### If U.S. Economy Collapses
- Apply to jobs in EU (Switzerland, UK, Germany, Ireland)
- Apply to jobs in Asia (Singapore, Japan, South Korea)
- Contract/freelance work from anywhere with internet
- **Realistic mobility time:** 1-3 months to become employable in new country

## SECTION 11: FORGOTTEN FACTORS

### Things not yet mentioned:

1. **Work-life balance:** Medical writers often have better work-life balance. Actuarial exam study requires 300-400 hours per exam for 7-10 exams. That is 2,100-4,000 hours of studying while working full-time.

2. **Age factor:** Starting actuarial exams at 31 (after graduation) is late. Most actuaries pass exams in their 20s. You will compete against younger candidates.

3. **Freelance potential:** Medical writers can freelance at $75-$150/hour. Actuaries rarely freelance.

4. **Remote work:** Medical writing is highly remote-friendly. Actuarial work is increasingly remote but historically office-based.

5. **Visa sponsorship if asylum denied:** 
   - Actuaries: Few companies sponsor internationally due to local credential requirements
   - Medical Writers: Global pharma companies regularly sponsor visas

6. **Career pivot options:**
   - Actuary: Can pivot to data science, risk management, finance
   - Medical Writer: Can pivot to clinical research, pharmacovigilance, medical affairs, scientific communications

7. **Automation risk (next 40 years):**
   - Actuarial: Medium-high. AI is already doing pricing models.
   - Medical Writing: Medium. AI assists but regulatory documents require human judgment and accountability.

8. **Burnout rates:**
   - Actuarial: Moderate. Exam stress is significant.
   - Medical Writing: Lower. Deadline-driven but manageable.
