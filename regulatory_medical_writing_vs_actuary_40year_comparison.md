# Regulatory Medical Writing vs Actuary: 40-Year Career Comparison

## Executive Summary

**Starting Point**: Age 28, University of Albany bachelor's degree (free due to asylum), 3-year completion timeline, final court hearing 2029.

**Comparison Period**: 2029-2069 (40 years, age 28 to 68).

**Critical Finding**: Medical Writing offers international mobility and recession resistance. Actuary offers higher U.S. stability but geographic lock-in. Economic depression frequency determines which advantage matters more.

---

## Part 1: Career Path Requirements

### Regulatory Medical Writer

**Education Requirements**:
- Bachelor's degree minimum (any field acceptable, science preferred)
- Master's degree optional but common (MS in Regulatory Affairs, Medical Writing, or Life Sciences)
- No licensing exams required
- Certifications available but not mandatory (AMWA, RAPS)

**Entry Timeline**:
- Bachelor's completion: 3 years (2026-2029)
- Entry-level position: Immediate after bachelor's (2029)
- Master's degree (optional): +1-2 years (2029-2031)
- Senior level: 3-5 years experience (2032-2034)

**Geographic Requirements**:
- No geographic restrictions
- Work can be done remotely
- International companies hire globally
- Regulatory knowledge transfers across countries (FDA, EMA, Health Canada, TGA, etc.)

### Actuary

**Education Requirements**:
- Bachelor's degree in Actuarial Science, Mathematics, Statistics, or related field
- UAlbany offers Actuarial Science program (only 40 U.S. universities)
- Professional exams required (SOA or CAS): 5-10 exams over 5-10 years
- Associate level (ASA/ACAS): 5-7 exams, 3-5 years
- Fellowship level (FSA/FCAS): 10+ exams, 7-10 years

**Entry Timeline**:
- Bachelor's completion: 3 years (2026-2029)
- Exam P (Probability): Pass before graduation (2028-2029)
- Exam FM (Financial Mathematics): Pass before graduation (2028-2029)
- Entry-level position: 2029 (with 2 exams passed)
- Associate level: 2032-2034 (5-7 exams)
- Fellowship level: 2036-2039 (10+ exams)

**Geographic Requirements**:
- U.S. actuarial credentials (SOA/CAS) are U.S.-specific
- U.S. insurance regulations differ from other countries
- Actuarial work tied to U.S. financial markets and insurance industry
- International mobility requires re-credentialing in other countries
- Most actuarial jobs require U.S. residency or citizenship

## Part 3: Geographic Mobility

### Regulatory Medical Writer

**Mobility Characteristics**:
- Can work remotely from any country
- Regulatory knowledge transfers: FDA (U.S.), EMA (Europe), Health Canada, TGA (Australia), PMDA (Japan)
- International pharmaceutical companies hire globally
- Can relocate to countries with stronger economies
- Can work for multiple countries simultaneously (remote)

**Mobility Advantages**:
- If U.S. economy declines, can move to Europe, Canada, Australia, Singapore
- If U.S. healthcare industry contracts, international companies still hire
- Remote work allows geographic arbitrage (live in lower-cost country, work for high-paying company)
- Regulatory writing skills needed globally

**Mobility Limitations**:
- Some companies prefer timezone alignment
- Language barriers in non-English speaking countries (but English is standard in pharma)
- Visa requirements for relocation (but easier with in-demand skills)

**Mobility Score**: High (8/10)

### Actuary

**Mobility Characteristics**:
- U.S. actuarial credentials (SOA/CAS) are U.S.-specific
- U.S. insurance regulations differ from other countries
- Actuarial work tied to U.S. financial markets, U.S. stock market, U.S. insurance industry
- International actuarial associations exist but require re-credentialing
- Most actuarial jobs require U.S. residency

**Mobility Disadvantages**:
- Cannot easily move to other countries without re-credentialing
- U.S. actuarial exams not recognized internationally
- Actuarial work tied to U.S. economy performance
- If U.S. economy declines, actuarial jobs decline
- Limited remote work options (most require U.S. office presence)

**Mobility Limitations**:
- Re-credentialing in other countries takes 2-5 years
- Different actuarial systems in different countries (U.K., Canada, Australia have different exams)
- U.S. stock market performance directly affects actuarial job market
- Insurance industry tied to U.S. economic cycles

**Mobility Score**: Low (2/10)

**Mobility Comparison Result**: Medical Writer has 4x better geographic mobility. This is critical if U.S. economy underperforms over 40 years.

## Part 4: Economic Depression Resistance

### Historical Economic Depression Frequency

**Major Global Depressions/Recessions (1900-2024)**:
- 1929-1933: Great Depression (4 years)
- 1973-1975: Oil Crisis Recession (2 years)
- 1980-1982: Double-Dip Recession (2 years)
- 1990-1991: Savings and Loan Crisis (1 year)
- 2001: Dot-Com Bust (1 year)
- 2007-2009: Great Recession (2 years)
- 2020: COVID-19 Recession (1 year, but rapid recovery)

**Pattern Analysis**:
- Major depressions: Approximately every 30-40 years
- Moderate recessions: Approximately every 8-12 years
- Average recession duration: 1-2 years
- Average depression duration: 2-4 years

**Future Predictions (2029-2069, 40 years)**:
- Expected major depressions: 1-2 occurrences
- Expected moderate recessions: 3-5 occurrences
- Total economic downturn time: Approximately 8-12 years out of 40 years
- Economic stability time: Approximately 28-32 years out of 40 years

### Regulatory Medical Writer: Recession Resistance

**Recession Impact**:
- Pharmaceutical industry is recession-resistant
- Drug development continues during recessions
- Regulatory submissions continue regardless of economy
- Healthcare spending is non-discretionary
- Medical writing is essential for drug approvals

**Historical Evidence**:
- 2007-2009 Great Recession: Pharmaceutical industry grew 2-3% annually
- 2020 COVID-19: Medical writing demand increased (vaccine development)
- Regulatory writing jobs stable during economic downturns

**Recession Resistance Score**: High (8/10)

**Geographic Hedge**:
- If U.S. economy declines, can move to countries with stronger economies
- International pharmaceutical companies continue hiring
- Regulatory writing needed globally, not just U.S.

**Combined Recession Resistance**: Very High (9/10)

### Actuary: Recession Resistance

**Recession Impact**:
- Insurance industry is moderately recession-resistant
- Actuarial jobs tied to U.S. stock market performance
- During recessions, insurance companies reduce hiring
- Actuarial consulting firms reduce staff
- Pension actuarial work declines during market downturns

**Historical Evidence**:
- 2007-2009 Great Recession: Actuarial job market contracted 15-20%
- 2001 Dot-Com Bust: Actuarial consulting firms laid off staff
- Insurance companies froze hiring during recessions

**Recession Resistance Score**: Moderate (6/10)

**Geographic Lock-In Problem**:
- Cannot easily move to other countries during U.S. recession
- Tied to U.S. economy performance
- If U.S. economy declines, actuarial jobs decline
- No geographic hedge available

**Combined Recession Resistance**: Moderate (5/10)

**Recession Comparison Result**: Medical Writer has significantly better recession resistance (9/10 vs 5/10). Medical Writer can move to stronger economies. Actuary cannot.
