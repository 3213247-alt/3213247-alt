### Projected 2031 Requirements:

The SOA/CAS have been increasing complexity:
- Exams are being restructured
- More emphasis on predictive analytics
- Competition intensifying

**2031 Entry-Level Competitive Threshold:**
- **Minimum to be considered**: 2 exams
- **Competitive without internship**: 4 exams
- **Standard expectation**: 3 exams + internship OR 4-5 exams without

**Will you need 4 exams by 2031?**
- With internship experience: 2-3 exams still viable
- Without internship experience: **YES, 4+ exams will likely be necessary to compensate**

## Part 4: The Real Numbers That Matter

### Why "No Internship" Is The Killer:

From industry data:
- 60-70% of entry-level actuarial hires come from internship conversions
- Of the remaining 30-40%, most are from target schools with recruiter relationships
- **Only 5-10% of entry-level hires are "cold applications" with no prior connection**

### Breaking Down That 5-10%:

Of those cold-hire positions:
- Most go to candidates with 4+ exams
- Most go to candidates with adjacent experience (underwriting, data analytics, risk)
- Very few go to pure graduates with zero experience

## Final Verdict

### The Honest Answer:

**University at Albany path with no internship:**
- Probability: **10-15% within 3 years**
- You're in the bottom half of candidates
- The 3 exams + technical skills help, but don't compensate for zero experience

**SUNY Empire path:**
- Probability: **7-12% within 3 years**
- More flexible but less competitive profile
- Would need exceptional self-driven networking

### The Math Reality:

With 3,000 candidates for 700 jobs:
- ~2,300 candidates will NOT get an actuarial job that year
- Those 2,300 either: pivot careers, try again next year (with worse odds), or get adjacent roles
- The pool refreshes annually with new graduates who have fresh internship experience

### The Strategic Truth:

If you cannot secure an internship, your best path is:
1. Pass as many exams as possible (4-5 before job hunting)
2. Get ANY insurance or data analytics work experience while studying
3. Network aggressively (actuarial societies, LinkedIn, informational interviews)
4. Be geographically flexible
5. Consider starting in adjacent roles (underwriting, data analyst at insurance company) and pivoting to actuarial

**Without these mitigations, your probability stays in the 5-15% range.**
