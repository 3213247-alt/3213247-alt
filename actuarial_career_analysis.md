## Part 1: Step-by-Step Hiring Probability Calculation

### Step 1: Define the Variables

**Given Data:**
- Total actuarial openings per year: 2,000
- New entry-level positions: 700
- Qualified candidates competing: 3,000 (constant each year)
- This means: 3,000 candidates compete for 700 positions annually

### Step 2: Base Probability Calculation

**Simple Probability Formula:**
```
Probability = (Number of Positions) / (Number of Candidates)
Probability = 700 / 3,000
Probability = 0.2333
Probability = 23.33%
```

**Base Probability: 23.3%**

### Step 3: Adjust for Qualification Levels

**Not all 3,000 candidates are equal. Typical distribution:**

1. **Highly Qualified (Top 20% = 600 candidates)**
   - 3-4 exams passed
   - Internship experience
   - Strong GPA (3.5+)
   - Technical skills (Python/R/SQL)
   - **Hiring probability for this group: 60-80%**

2. **Moderately Qualified (Middle 50% = 1,500 candidates)**
   - 1-2 exams passed
   - Some projects/skills
   - Variable GPA
   - **Hiring probability for this group: 15-25%**

3. **Minimally Qualified (Bottom 30% = 900 candidates)**
   - 0-1 exams passed
   - No internship
   - Limited skills
   - **Hiring probability for this group: 2-5%**

### Step 4: Calculate Your Specific Probability

**Your Profile (Path 1: University of Albany):**
- Bachelor's degree: Actuarial Science + Math
- Exams: 1 exam initially, then 2 more later (total 3 exams)
- Skills: Python, R, SQL
- Projects: Small projects completed
- **Experience: NONE**
- **Internship: NONE**

**Your Profile Classification:**
- **Exams**: 1-3 exams = Moderately Qualified
- **Skills**: Python/R/SQL = Above average for entry-level
- **Experience**: None = Major disadvantage
- **Internship**: None = Major disadvantage

**Your Adjusted Probability Calculation:**

**Base calculation:**
- You're in the "Moderately Qualified" group (1,500 candidates)
- But missing internship = significant disadvantage
- Missing experience = significant disadvantage

**Probability Adjustment Factors:**
- Base probability: 23.3%
- Without internship: -8% to -12%
- Without experience: -5% to -8%
- With technical skills (Python/R/SQL): +3% to +5%
- With 3 exams: +2% to +4%

**Your Estimated Probability Range:**
- **Optimistic**: 23.3% - 8% - 5% + 5% + 4% = **19.3%**
- **Realistic**: 23.3% - 10% - 6% + 4% + 3% = **14.3%**
- **Pessimistic**: 23.3% - 12% - 8% + 3% + 2% = **8.3%**

**YOUR PROBABILITY RANGE: 8-20%**

### Step 5: Market Competition Reality Check

**Additional Factors Reducing Probability:**

1. **Geographic Concentration**
   - Most jobs in major cities (NYC, Chicago, Hartford, etc.)
   - If you're not in these areas: -3% to -5%

2. **Timing**
   - Most hiring happens in fall (for next year's grads)
   - Off-cycle applications: -2% to -4%

3. **Network Effects**
   - Referrals significantly increase probability
   - Without network: -2% to -3%

4. **Resume Screening**
   - ATS systems filter out 70-80% of resumes
   - Without internship keyword: -5% to -8%

**Final Adjusted Probability Range:**

**Conservative Estimate:**
- Base: 14.3%
- Geographic disadvantage: -4%
- Timing disadvantage: -3%
- Network disadvantage: -2%
- Resume screening: -6%
- **FINAL: 8-12%**

**Optimistic Estimate:**
- Base: 19.3%
- Geographic advantage: +0%
- Good timing: +0%
- Network neutral: +0%
- Resume passes screening: +0%
- **FINAL: 15-20%**

### Step 6: Final Probability Range

**YOUR LIKELIHOOD OF GETTING HIRED: 8-20%**

**Breakdown:**
- **Best case scenario**: 20% (1 in 5 chance)
- **Realistic scenario**: 12-15% (1 in 7 to 1 in 8 chance)
- **Worst case scenario**: 8% (1 in 12.5 chance)

**This means:**
- You would need to apply to **5-12 positions** to have a reasonable chance of getting one offer
- With 700 positions available, you'd need to be in the top 14-20% of candidates to secure a position

## Part 3: Exam Requirements by 2031

### Current Exam Requirements (2024)

**Entry-Level Positions:**
- **Minimum**: 1-2 exams (Exam P and/or Exam FM)
- **Competitive**: 2-3 exams
- **Highly competitive**: 3-4 exams

**Associate Level (ASA/ACAS):**
- **SOA**: 7 exams + modules + VEE credits
- **CAS**: 7 exams + modules + VEE credits

### Historical Trend Analysis

**Exam Requirements Over Time:**
- **2000s**: 1-2 exams for entry-level
- **2010s**: 2-3 exams for entry-level
- **2020s**: 2-3 exams for entry-level (some requiring 3-4)

**Market Saturation Effect:**
- As more candidates enter market, requirements increase
- With 3,000 qualified candidates for 700 positions, competition drives requirements up

### Projection for 2031 (7 years from now)

**Factors Increasing Requirements:**
1. **Market saturation**: More candidates = higher bar
2. **Technology changes**: More technical skills needed
3. **Industry evolution**: Actuarial work becoming more technical

**Factors Maintaining Current Requirements:**
1. **Exam difficulty**: Exams are already challenging
2. **Time constraints**: Students can only pass so many exams
3. **Industry needs**: Companies still need entry-level workers

### 2031 Projection

**Entry-Level Requirements by 2031:**
- **Minimum**: 2 exams (up from 1-2 currently)
- **Competitive**: 3-4 exams (up from 2-3 currently)
- **Highly competitive**: 4-5 exams (up from 3-4 currently)

**Answer: YES, 4 exams will likely be needed for competitive entry-level positions by 2031**

**However:**
- 2-3 exams will still get you interviews (just lower probability)
- 4 exams will be the "sweet spot" for competitive candidates
- 5+ exams may be overqualified for entry-level (some employers avoid this)

**Your Path:**
- You plan to finish 3 exams = **Below the 2031 competitive standard**
- This reduces your probability by additional 3-5%
- **Adjusted probability with 3 exams in 2031: 5-15%**
