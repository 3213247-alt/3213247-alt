## PATH B: INDUSTRIAL ELECTRICIAN

### MONTH 1 (JANUARY 2026): RESEARCH & PREPARE

**Week 1-2: Learn the basics**
- Research: What industrial electricians do (vs residential)
- Focus: Commercial buildings, data centers, manufacturing, hospitals
- Watch: YouTube videos on electrical apprenticeship programs
- **Goal**: Understand the career path

**Week 3-4: Physical preparation**
- Industrial electrical work is physically demanding
- Requirements: Able to lift 50+ lbs, work on ladders, work in confined spaces
- Color vision: Must be able to distinguish wire colors (red, black, white, green)
- If you have back problems, knee problems, or fear of heights → reconsider

**Research apprenticeship programs:**

**Option 1: IBEW (International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers) - Union**
- Advantages: Higher pay, better benefits, pension, strong job security
- Disadvantages: Union dues, must follow union rules
- Application: Apply directly to local IBEW chapter

**Option 2: ABC (Associated Builders and Contractors) - Non-union**
- Advantages: Faster advancement, more flexibility
- Disadvantages: Lower pay than union (typically), no pension
- Application: Apply directly to local ABC chapter

**Option 3: Direct employer apprenticeship**
- Large electrical contractors offer their own programs
- Advantages: Immediate employment
- Disadvantages: Variable quality, less standardized

**Target local IBEW chapters (if in these cities):**
- New York: IBEW Local 3
- Chicago: IBEW Local 134
- Los Angeles: IBEW Local 11
- Boston: IBEW Local 103
- Philadelphia: IBEW Local 98
- San Francisco: IBEW Local 6
- Seattle: IBEW Local 46
- Denver: IBEW Local 68

### MONTH 2 (FEBRUARY 2026): APPLY TO APPRENTICESHIPS

**Application requirements (typical):**
- High school diploma or GED
- Driver's license (valid)
- Age 18+ (you're 28, this is fine)
- Pass drug test
- Pass aptitude test (algebra, reading comprehension)

**Aptitude test preparation:**
- Math: Basic algebra (solve for X, fractions, decimals, percentages, basic geometry)
- Reading: Comprehension of technical instructions
- Study: Khan Academy math refresher (free online)
- Practice: IBEW aptitude test practice questions (Google "IBEW aptitude test practice")
- Study time: 2-3 weeks, 1-2 hours per day

**Application process:**
1. Apply online to local IBEW or ABC chapter
2. Take aptitude test (usually scheduled 2-4 weeks after application)
3. Interview (if pass aptitude test)
4. Wait for acceptance (can take 2-6 months)
5. Start apprenticeship (typically fall intake, but varies)

**Interview preparation:**
- Why do you want to be an electrician? "I enjoy hands-on work, problem-solving, and building things that last. Electrical work is critical infrastructure, and I want to develop a skilled trade."
- What's your experience with tools/construction? (Any DIY projects, car repairs, building experience counts)
- Are you comfortable with physical work? "Yes, I understand this is physically demanding and I'm prepared for that."
- Are you reliable? (They care a lot about attendance, being on time)

### MONTHS 3-6 (MARCH-JUNE 2026): ACCEPTANCE & START

**Expected timeline:**
- Application: February 2026
- Aptitude test: March 2026
- Interview: April 2026
- Acceptance: May-June 2026
- Start date: Summer or Fall 2026 (many programs start in September)
- **Your age at start: 28.5-29 years old**

**What to expect Year 1 apprentice:**
- Pay: $35K-$45K (Year 1 apprentice is typically 40-50% of journeyman wage)
- Hours: 40 hours/week on-job + 4-8 hours/week classroom (usually evening classes)
- Work: Assisting journeymen, running wire, basic installations
- Tools: You'll need basic tools ($500-$1,000 initial investment; often reimbursed or provided by employer)

### LONG-TERM PATH (YEARS 1-25)

**Years 1-4: Apprentice**
- Year 1: $35K-$45K (40-50% of journeyman rate)
- Year 2: $40K-$50K (50-60% of journeyman rate)
- Year 3: $45K-$55K (60-70% of journeyman rate)
- Year 4: $50K-$65K (70-90% of journeyman rate)
- Learning: Electrical theory, code (NEC), installation, troubleshooting
- Classroom: 600-900 hours total over 4 years

**Year 4-5: Journeyman Electrician (age 32-33)**
- Pass journeyman exam
- Salary: $55K-$70K
- Independent work (no longer need supervision)
- Can work anywhere as licensed journeyman

**Years 5-10: Specialize**
- Focus on: Industrial (manufacturing, refineries) or Commercial (data centers, hospitals, high-rises)
- Avoid: Residential (lower pay, less stable)
- Salary: $70K-$90K
- Build expertise in specific systems (high-voltage, controls, automation)

**Years 10-15: Master Electrician**
- Pass master electrician exam (requirements vary by state)
- Salary: $85K-$110K
- Can supervise others, pull permits, run jobs
- Option: Start own electrical contracting business

**Years 15-25: Peak Career**
- Continue as master electrician or business owner
- Salary: $90K-$130K+ (more if own business)
- Option: Transition to estimating/project management (less physical as you age)
- **Total career earnings: $2.1M-$2.5M**

## WHAT NOT TO DO

### ❌ DO NOT: Apply to software engineering bootcamps
- Reason: Age 28 + 6 months bootcamp = 28.5, then 6-12 months job search = 29-30 entry
- Problem: Tech industry has strong age bias against 30+ entry-level engineers
- Job market: Oversaturated (10:1 application ratios typical)
- Verdict: Wrong path for age 28 start

### ❌ DO NOT: Apply to management consulting firms
- Reason: MBB and Big 4 prefer <28 entry for analyst roles
- Problem: Extreme hours (60-70), poor retention (3-7 years), up-or-out culture
- Verdict: Fails ageism and retention requirements

### ❌ DO NOT: Start actuarial exams directly (without job)
- Reason: Age 28 + 2-3 years exams = 30-31 entry, competition ratio 6:1 by 2030
- Problem: Low success rate (30-40%) for entry-level at that age
- Alternative: Get insurance analyst job FIRST, then do exams with company support
- Verdict: Too risky as primary plan

### ❌ DO NOT: Apply to data science bootcamps
- Reason: Market oversaturated, same age bias as software engineering
- Problem: "Data scientist" title inflated; many roles are actually data analyst
- Verdict: Poor ROI given current market saturation

### ❌ DO NOT: Go back for 4-year bachelor's degree
- Reason: Age 28 + 4 years = 32, severe ageism
- Problem: $40K-$80K cost, 4 years opportunity cost
- Alternative: If you need degree, get associate's degree (2 years) in specific field or targeted certifications
- Verdict: Wrong strategy for age 28 with time constraints

### ❌ DO NOT: "Keep options open" or delay decision past March 2026
- Reason: Every month delayed = older at employment
- Problem: Age 28 is already late for some fields; age 29+ is worse
- Verdict: Decide and act within 90 days (January-March 2026)
