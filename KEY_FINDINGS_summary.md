## THE BIG ERRORS (Ranked by Impact)

### 🔴 ERROR #1: Supply Calculation Off by **21x**

**Claimed**: 80-100 people with 3-4 exams + tech + communication
**Reality**: 2,100 people with 3-4 exams + tech + communication

**Why?**
- Used single-sitting pass rates (48%) instead of cumulative rates (65-70%)
- Assumed too-high attrition (60-70%) instead of reality (75-85%)
- Errors compounded across 4 exam stages

**Impact**: Made market look undersupplied when it's actually oversupplied

---

### 🔴 ERROR #2: Demand Calculation Off by **2.1x**

**Claimed**: 1,000 entry-level jobs
**Reality**: 480 entry-level jobs

**Why?**
- Assumed 30-40% of openings are entry-level
- Reality: Only 15-25% are entry-level
- Most openings are senior replacements (retirements)

**Impact**: Made market look wide open when it's actually very tight

---

### 🔴 ERROR #3: Market Dynamics Completely Inverted

**Claimed**: "Supply shortage - candidates have great odds"
**Reality**: "Supply glut - fierce competition"

**The Math**:
```
CLAIMED RATIO (2025):
80 candidates ÷ 1,000 jobs = 0.08 candidates per job
= Supply shortage = Employers desperate

ACTUAL RATIO (2025):
2,100 candidates ÷ 480 jobs = 4.4 candidates per job
= Supply glut = Candidates desperate
```

**Impact**: Completely wrong understanding of market conditions

---

### 🟡 ERROR #4: Ignored the Internship Filter

**Missing from analysis**: 80% of entry-level hires have internships

**Reality**:
- 2,100 people with exams + tech + communication
- ~1,470 of those also have internships (70%)
- ~480 jobs available
- **1,470 ÷ 480 = 3.1 candidates per job (among those with internships)**

**Without internship**: Success rate drops from 37% to 5-10%

**Impact**: Internships are THE primary filter, not exams
