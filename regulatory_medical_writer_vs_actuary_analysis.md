# Regulatory Medical Writer vs. Actuary: 40-Year Career Comparison

## Starting Conditions
- **Age at start:** 28 years old
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree from University at Albany, New York (free via asylum status)
- **Career endpoint:** Age 68 (2025-2065)
- **Location:** United States

---

## PART 1: ENTRY REQUIREMENTS

### Regulatory Medical Writer

**Minimum Requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree in life sciences, pharmacy, or related field
- Entry possible with bachelor's degree
- No mandatory certifications to start
- Optional: AMWA (American Medical Writers Association) certification later

**Time to First Job:** 6-18 months after degree
- Requires building writing portfolio
- May need 1-2 internships or contract positions first

**Reality Check:** A general bachelor's degree may require additional coursework in biology, pharmacology, or clinical research to be competitive.

---

### Actuary

**Minimum Requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree (any field, but math/statistics preferred)
- Must pass actuarial exams (SOA or CAS)
- Exam progression: 7-10 exams over 5-10 years

**Time to First Job:** Can start as "actuarial analyst" while passing exams
- Exam 1 (Probability) and Exam 2 (Financial Mathematics) needed for entry-level
- Each exam requires 300-400 hours of study
- Pass rates: 30-50% per exam

**Reality Check:** At age 28, starting exams means Associate status (ASA) by age 32-34, Fellow status (FSA) by age 35-38. This is late compared to typical actuaries who start at 22.

## PART 8: CRITICAL DECISION FACTORS

### Factor 1: Exam Risk

**Actuary Exam Reality:**
- 10-15% of people who start actuarial exams achieve FSA
- Median time to FSA: 7-10 years
- Starting at 28 means competing with 22-year-olds who have more study time
- Each failed exam costs: $1,000-2,000 (fees) + 300-400 hours + 6 months delay
- Employers may terminate after multiple failures

**Medical Writer Certification Reality:**
- No mandatory exams
- AMWA certification is optional, not career-limiting if skipped
- Career advancement based on portfolio and experience
- No exam-failure career death spiral

**Risk Assessment:** Actuary carries significant exam-failure career risk. Medical writing does not.

---

### Factor 2: Work-Life Balance

**Medical Writer:**
- Standard 40-45 hour weeks
- Deadline crunches exist (regulatory submissions)
- Freelance option allows control
- Remote work highly available (80%+ of roles)

**Actuary:**
- Standard 40-50 hour weeks
- Quarterly reporting crunches
- Year-end peaks
- Study time for exams: 15-25 hours/week for 5-10 years ON TOP of work
- Remote work increasing but still ~60% of roles

**Total Time Investment Difference:**
- Actuary exams require 3,000-5,000 additional hours over 10 years
- This equals 1.5-2.5 years of full-time work unpaid

---

### Factor 3: Career Ceiling Without Advanced Degree

**Medical Writer:**
- Bachelor's degree sufficient for director level
- Master's/PhD helps for VP level but not required
- No hard credential barrier

**Actuary:**
- FSA is the credential, not graduate degree
- Advanced degree optional
- FSA is hard barrier to senior roles

---

### Factor 4: Industry Disruption Risk (AI/Automation)

**Medical Writer (AI Impact):**
- AI drafting tools emerging (2024-2025)
- Will change workflow, not eliminate jobs
- FDA requires human accountability for submissions
- Prediction: 20-30% productivity increase, not job loss
- Writers who use AI will replace writers who don't

**Actuary (AI Impact):**
- Actuarial modeling already heavily automated
- AI enhancing predictive models
- Regulatory sign-off still requires human actuary
- Prediction: 10-20% productivity increase
- Entry-level analyst work most at risk

**Verdict:** Both careers have regulatory protection against full automation. Neither faces existential AI threat.
